## Finding 1: Tipping Point Cascades Are Destabilizing (2024)

**Source:** Wunderling et al. (2024) - "Climate tipping point interactions and cascades: a review"
**Journal:** Earth System Dynamics 15:41-74
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-15-41-2024
**Type:** Comprehensive peer-reviewed review

### Key Findings

**On Cascade Interactions:**
> "We find indications that **many of the interactions between tipping elements are destabilizing**."

**On 1.5-2°C Warming:**
> "Tipping cascades **cannot be ruled out** on centennial to millennial timescales at global warming levels between 1.5 and 2.0 °C or on shorter timescales if global warming surpassed 2.0 °C."

**On Fast Cascades:**
> At higher warming levels, "tipping cascades may include **fast tipping elements such as the AMOC or the Amazon rainforest**."

### Analysis

This 2024 comprehensive review directly contradicts the simulation's stability claims:

1. **Destabilizing > Stabilizing:** Most interactions push systems TOWARD tipping, not away from it
2. **No safety at 1.5-2°C:** Cascades possible even within Paris Agreement targets
3. **Acceleration beyond 2°C:** Fast tipping elements activate at higher warming

**Verdict:** ❌ **This paper provides ZERO support for self-limiting mechanisms**. It warns about cascading destabilization.

## Finding 2: Negative Feedbacks Weakening (2024-2025)

**Source:** Multiple 2024-2025 climate feedback studies
**Context:** Net climate feedback becoming less negative

### Key Findings

**On Overall Feedback Trend:**
> "While the overall sum of feedbacks is negative, **it is becoming less negative as greenhouse gas emissions continue**, meaning that warming is slower than it would be in the absence of feedbacks, but warming will accelerate if emissions continue at current levels."

**On Planck Feedback:**
The Planck response (thermal radiation increasing with temperature) is "the most fundamental feedback in the climate system" and remains strongly negative. However, other feedbacks (clouds, water vapor, ice-albedo) are reducing the net stabilizing effect.

### Analysis

**Planck Feedback:** Yes, the Earth radiates more heat as it warms (Stefan-Boltzmann law). This is a genuine negative feedback.

**However:**
1. This does NOT create a "5% stability floor" after crossing tipping points
2. The net feedback is becoming LESS stabilizing over time
3. Planck feedback operates continuously, not as a "floor" mechanism
4. Other positive feedbacks (methane release, ice loss, forest dieback) can overwhelm Planck response

**Verdict:** ⚠️ **Planck feedback exists but does NOT support a stability floor after tipping cascades**

## Finding 3: Permafrost Feedback (2025)

**Source:** "Permafrost response and feedback under temperature stabilization and overshoot scenarios"
**Journal:** Earth System Dynamics 16:1809-1830 (2025)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-16-1809-2025

### Key Findings

**On Permafrost Tipping:**
> "Feedback factors for stabilization scenarios show values of approximately 0.06 °C °C−1 by 2300, with findings indicating that the **positive permafrost carbon feedback is unlikely to result in enough additional thawing to initiate a self-perpetuating tipping process**."

### Analysis

This is a RARE example of limited self-perpetuation, but:

1. **Applies ONLY to permafrost**, not other tipping elements
2. **Requires temperature stabilization** (Paris Agreement success scenario)
3. **By 2300** - extremely long timescale
4. **Does NOT prevent initial tipping** - just limits runaway cascade

**Verdict:** ⚠️ **Very limited stabilization mechanism, does NOT generalize to 5% floor claim**

## Finding 4: Climate Stabilization Pathways (2024)

**Source:** "Exploring climate stabilisation at different global warming levels in ACCESS-ESM-1.5"
**Journal:** Earth System Dynamics 15:1353-1383 (2024)
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-15-1353-2024

### Key Findings

**On Paris Agreement Scenarios:**
Study includes seven 1000-year simulations with temperatures stabilizing at 1.5°C, 2°C, and higher warming levels.

### Analysis

These are **POLICY scenarios** (achieving net-zero emissions), NOT evidence of natural self-limiting mechanisms:

1. Stabilization requires human intervention (net-zero CO₂)
2. Without emissions cuts, warming continues
3. Models show stability IF policy succeeds, not natural floors

**Verdict:** ❌ **This is policy-driven stabilization, NOT self-limiting natural feedback**

## Critical Analysis: Why 5% Floor Is Not Research-Backed

### 1. Confusion Between Continuous Feedback and Stability Floor

**Planck Feedback** (thermal radiation) is real and operates continuously:
- As T increases → radiation increases (T⁴ relationship)
- This slows warming but does NOT create a "floor" after tipping

**The simulation's 5% floor** suggests:
- Even after crossing multiple tipping points
- Even with cascading destabilization
- Earth retains some irreducible minimum stability

**These are DIFFERENT concepts:**
- Continuous negative feedback ≠ Stability floor after collapse
- Slowing warming ≠ Preventing complete destabilization

### 2. Tipping Cascades Can Overwhelm Negative Feedbacks

Wunderling et al. (2024) show:
- AMOC collapse → Greenland ice loss acceleration
- Amazon dieback → Atmospheric circulation changes
- Permafrost thaw → Methane release → Warming acceleration

**Cascades create NEW positive feedbacks** that can exceed Planck response.

### 3. Timescale Mismatch

**Geological recovery** (cited in original code, Zachos 2008 PETM):
- 200,000 years to recover from +5-8°C
- This is NOT a "self-limiting mechanism" on human timescales

**Policy-relevant timescales** (2025-2100):
- Cascades possible on centennial scales (Wunderling 2024)
- Acceleration beyond 2°C (multiple studies)
- No 200ky available for recovery

### 4. Recent Research Emphasizes Risk, Not Stability

**Language in 2024-2025 papers:**
- "Planet on the brink" (BioScience 2025)
- "Cascades cannot be ruled out" (Wunderling 2024)
- "Warming possibly accelerating" (State of Climate 2025)
- "Many interactions are destabilizing" (Wunderling 2024)

**NOT:**
- "Self-limiting mechanisms preserve stability"
- "5% minimum stability floor"
- "Earth systems remain habitable after tipping"
