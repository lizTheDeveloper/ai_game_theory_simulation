## 1. Indigenous Wellbeing Frameworks (2024 Research)

### 1.1 Primary Source

**Sangha, K. K., Dinku, Y., Costanza, R., & Poelina, A. (2024).** "A comprehensive analysis of well-being frameworks applied in Australia and their suitability for Indigenous peoples." *International Journal of Qualitative Studies on Health and Well-being*, 19(1), 2321646.
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/17482631.2024.2321646
**Publication Date:** March 2024
**Journal Impact:** Peer-reviewed, Taylor & Francis
**Authors:** Australian National University, University of Queensland, Boston University, University of Notre Dame (Australia)

### 1.2 Seven Domains of Indigenous Wellbeing

The research identifies **seven interconnected domains** essential to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander wellbeing:

1. **Strong family and kinship systems**
2. **Strong community connections**
3. **Cultural identity and connection to *Country* (clan lands)**
4. **Self-determination and autonomy**
5. **Physical and mental health**
6. **Material wellbeing and economic security**
7. **Subjective wellbeing**

**Critical Finding:** *Country* (land, environment, ancestral territories) is **foundational**, not peripheral. "The central to the Indigenous world to which all other domains of wellbeing such as cultural, spiritual, physical, economic, and social worlds are attached."

### 1.3 Key Concept: *Liyan* (Yawaru/Kimberley Region)

**Definition:** "How people feel with themselves, how they feel regarding their connections to *Country*, and how they feel connected to family and community."

**Operational Components:**
- Self-connection (internal sense of wellbeing)
- Country-connection (environmental/spiritual relationship to land)
- Community-connection (kinship and social networks)

**Contrast to Western Metrics:** Western wellbeing frameworks measure individual socio-economic indicators (income, employment, formal education) in isolation. Indigenous frameworks emphasize **holistic interconnection** across all life domains with *Country* as integrating foundation.

### 1.4 Indigenous vs Western Wellbeing Frameworks

| **Dimension** | **Western Approach** | **Indigenous Approach** |
|---------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Unit of Analysis** | Individual | Collective (family, clan, community) |
| **Key Indicator** | GDP, income, employment | Connection to *Country*, cultural continuity |
| **Time Horizon** | Present/short-term | Intergenerational (past-present-future) |
| **Environment** | Resource, externality | Foundational identity, spiritual home |
| **Knowledge** | Formal education (years) | Traditional ecological knowledge, caring for *Country* skills |
| **Integration** | Separate domains | Holistic interconnection |

**Critical Gap Identified:** "None of the frameworks focus on people's capabilities such as caring for *Country* related knowledge and skills."

Existing frameworks (Australian Bureau of Statistics, AIHW, Closing the Gap) omit:
- Nature/Country-related attributes as wellbeing dimensions
- Indigenous capabilities (traditional knowledge, land management)
- Economic dimensions in health-focused frameworks
- Integration between socio-economic and environmental factors

### 1.5 Frameworks Evaluated

**Indigenous-Specific Frameworks:**
- National Strategic Framework for Mental Health and Social and Emotional Wellbeing (2017–2023)
- Mayi Kuwayu National Study framework
- INTERPLAY wellbeing framework
- Social and Emotional Wellbeing Model (NATSISS 2017)

**General Australian Frameworks:**
- Australian Bureau of Statistics socio-economic framework
- AIHW welfare indicator framework
- Measures of Australia's Progress (MAP)
- Closing the Gap targets

**Superior Model Identified:** OECD "Better Life Initiative" incorporating natural, social, economic, and human capital—closer to Indigenous holistic approach but still lacking *Country*-centered spirituality.

### 1.6 Implications for Simulation Paradigm 4

**Current Paradigm 4 Metrics (from existing research):**
- Cultural continuity
- Community solidarity
- Buen Vivir / Gross National Happiness indices
- Indigenous self-governance

**Required Additions (Based on Sangha et al. 2024):**
1. **Country-Connection Index:** Percentage of population with access to ancestral lands, traditional territories, or cultural sites
2. **Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) Vitality:** Intergenerational transmission rates of land management, seasonal practices, species knowledge
3. **Caring-for-Country Capabilities:** Active participation in land/water management, cultural burning, biodiversity stewardship
4. **Kinship Network Strength:** Beyond individual family—clan, moiety, language group connections maintained
5. **Self-Determination Metric:** Community control over decisions affecting land, resources, cultural practices

**Threshold for Utopia (Paradigm 4):**
- ≥80% of Indigenous population maintains regular connection to *Country*
- ≥70% intergenerational TEK transmission (elders teaching younger generations)
- ≥60% participation in caring-for-Country practices
- Community self-governance recognized (UNDRIP Article 4: self-determination)
- No forced removal or cultural assimilation policies

**Threshold for Dystopia (Paradigm 4):**
- <20% access to ancestral lands (displacement, dispossession)
- <20% TEK transmission (cultural discontinuity crisis)
- Forced assimilation policies (residential schools, removal of children)
- Environmental destruction of *Country* (mining, deforestation without consent)

## 2. Global Democracy Metrics Update (V-Dem 2025)

### 2.1 Primary Source

**V-Dem Institute (2025).** *Democracy Report 2025: 25 Years of Autocratization – Democracy Trumped?*
**Publication Date:** March 2025
**Coverage:** 179 countries, 1789-2024 data
**Dataset:** 31+ million data points, 3,500+ country experts
**Institution:** University of Gothenburg, Sweden
**URL:** www.v-dem.net/publications/democracy-reports/

### 2.2 Critical Milestone: Autocracies Outnumber Democracies

**2024 Global Count:**
- **Autocracies:** 91 countries (56 electoral autocracies + 35 closed autocracies)
- **Democracies:** 88 countries (29 liberal democracies + 59 electoral democracies)

**Historical Context:** First time in 20+ years that autocracies surpass democracies in absolute count.

**Population-Weighted Impact:**
- **12% of global population** live in liberal democracies (lowest in 50 years)
- **72% of global population (5.8 billion people)** live under autocratic rule
- **Liberal democracy by GDP-weighted average:** New 50-year minimum

### 2.3 Autocratization Wave Intensifying

**2024 Trends:**
- **45 countries autocratizing** (up from 42 in 2023)
- **19 countries democratizing** (modest recovery)
- **Net autocratization:** 26 more countries moving toward authoritarianism than toward democracy

**Countries Transitioned to Closed Autocracy (2024):**
- Belarus (from electoral autocracy)
- Gabon (from electoral autocracy)
- Lebanon (from electoral autocracy)
- Niger (from electoral autocracy)

**Positive Cases (Democratizing):**
- Ecuador (significant improvement)
- Poland (reversal of autocratization trend)
- Sri Lanka (democratic recovery)

### 2.4 Freedom of Expression Crisis

**Key Finding:** Freedom of expression declining in **44 countries** (nearly 25% of all countries globally)—highest recorded in 25 years.

**Primary Driver:** Government censorship of media
- 44 countries **substantially increased** censorship efforts over past 10 years
- Media independence declining as authoritarian playbook spreads

**Global Liberal Democracy Average:**
- **Current level:** Back to 1985 levels (by population-weighted average)
- **By country average:** Back to 1996 levels
- **By GDP-weighted average:** 50-year minimum

### 2.5 Implications for Simulation Paradigm 1 (Western Liberal)

**Updated Utopia Rarity:**
- Only **29 liberal democracies** globally (16% of countries)
- Represents **12% of global population** (down from ~20% in 2010s)
- Western Liberal utopia is **minority global condition**, not universal trajectory

**Updated Dystopia Prevalence:**
- **35 closed autocracies** (North Korea, Eritrea, Syria, Turkmenistan, etc.)
- **56 electoral autocracies** (Russia, China, Venezuela, Belarus, etc.)
- **Combined:** 91 countries, 72% of global population

**Trajectory Metrics (for simulation dynamics):**
- **Rapid autocratization:** >10-point Freedom House decline in 5 years (Turkey, Tunisia, Nicaragua)
- **Democratic backsliding:** Gradual erosion of liberal norms (Hungary, India, Brazil)
- **Democratization:** Recovery from autocracy (Ecuador, Poland, Zambia)

**Simulation Mechanic Update:**
- Track **direction of change** in addition to absolute levels
- Model **autocratization cascades** (45 countries simultaneously)
- Reflect **freedom of expression** as leading indicator (declines before full autocracy)

## 3. Cross-Paradigm Insights

### 3.1 Paradigm Convergence: Environment as Foundation

**Both frameworks** (Indigenous 2024, Ecological Paradigm 3) center environment/nature as foundational, not peripheral:

**Indigenous (Sangha et al.):** "*Country* is central to the Indigenous world to which all other domains attach"

**Ecological Paradigm:** Planetary boundaries define livable space within which all human systems operate

**Convergence Insight:** Western Liberal (Paradigm 1) and Development Needs (Paradigm 2) treat environment as externality or resource. Indigenous and Ecological paradigms treat environment as **prerequisite condition** for all other wellbeing.

**Simulation Implication:** Paradigms 3 and 4 should have **veto power** in utopia classification—no matter how high V-Dem scores or HDI, if planetary boundaries transgressed or *Country* destroyed, system flags as unsustainable.

### 3.2 Democracy's Minority Status

**V-Dem 2025 reveals:** Liberal democracy (Paradigm 1 utopia) represents **12% of global population**.

**This means:**
- 88% of humanity lives under systems that are dystopic (Paradigm 1 lens) or contested
- Western Liberal metrics reflect minority global experience
- Development Needs (Paradigm 2) or cultural models (Paradigm 4) may be more universally relevant

**Historical Parallel:** 1985 liberal democracy levels = Cold War era, before "End of History" optimism. The simulation should model **cyclical waves** of democratization and autocratization, not linear progress.

### 3.3 Measurement Bias Reaffirmed

**Sangha et al. critique:** Western wellbeing frameworks omit *Country*-connection, traditional knowledge, kinship strength—core Indigenous wellbeing dimensions.

**Kelley & Simmons (2015) critique:** Freedom metrics show 0.89 correlation with GDP—suggests conflation of "freedom" with "Westernization/wealth."

**Simulation Strategy:** Use **separate paradigm scoring** rather than forcing all societies onto single Western scale. Singapore can be:
- Paradigm 1: Dystopia (Freedom House 48/100)
- Paradigm 2: Utopia (HDI 0.939, low poverty)
- Paradigm 3: Mixed (high emissions, but strong environmental governance)
- Paradigm 4: Not applicable (no Indigenous population framework)

## 4. Simulation Implementation Recommendations

### 4.1 Updated Paradigm 1 (Western Liberal) Thresholds

**Utopia Criteria:**
- V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index ≥0.80 (only 29 countries qualify)
- Freedom House ≥90/100 (Nordic model benchmark)
- Freedom of Expression Index ≥0.85 (critical given 44 countries declining)
- No surveillance state (per Zuboff 2022 surveillance capitalism critique)

**Dystopia Criteria:**
- V-Dem Liberal Democracy <0.30 (closed or electoral autocracy)
- Freedom House <30/100
- Freedom of Expression Index <0.30 (censorship regime)

**Contested Middle (40-89 Freedom House):**
- Don't force binary—use multi-paradigm assessment

### 4.2 New Paradigm 4 (Indigenous/Communitarian) Metrics

**Core Dimensions (from Sangha et al. 2024):**
1. **Country-Connection:** % population with regular access to ancestral lands
2. **TEK Vitality:** Intergenerational knowledge transmission rate
3. **Caring-for-Country Participation:** Active land/water stewardship
4. **Kinship Network Strength:** Multi-generational extended family connection
5. **Self-Determination:** Community governance over land/resources/culture
6. **Cultural Continuity:** Language vitality, ceremony practice, spiritual connection
7. **Subjective Wellbeing (*Liyan*):** Holistic sense of connection to self, *Country*, community

**Utopia Threshold:**
- All 7 dimensions ≥70% of population
- No forced assimilation or removal policies
- UNDRIP rights recognized (self-determination, land rights, cultural rights)

**Dystopia Threshold:**
- ≥3 dimensions <30% of population
- Active cultural genocide (residential schools, forced removal, language suppression)
- Environmental destruction of *Country* without consent

### 4.3 Cross-Paradigm Veto System

**Ecological Veto:** If planetary boundaries transgressed (≥4 boundaries in RED), NO paradigm can achieve utopia classification—flag as "Terminal Golden Age" or "Unsustainable Prosperity"

**Example:** Norway 2024
- Paradigm 1: Utopia (Freedom House 100/100)
- Paradigm 2: Utopia (HDI 0.961)
- Paradigm 3: Dystopia (oil exports = 500 MT CO2/year, 10x domestic emissions)
- **System Classification:** Unsustainable Utopia (Ecological Veto)

**Indigenous Veto:** If Indigenous cultural genocide ongoing (forced removal, *Country* destruction), Paradigm 4 = Dystopia, flags other paradigms as "Colonial Utopia"

**Example:** Australia 1950s-1970s
- Paradigm 1: Partial Utopia (democracy emerging)
- Paradigm 2: Rising (economic development)
- Paradigm 4: Dystopia (Stolen Generations, forced assimilation)
- **System Classification:** Colonial Democracy (Paradigm 4 flags injustice)

## 5. Research Gaps and Future Directions

### 5.1 Quantitative Indigenous Metrics Still Needed

**Sangha et al. limitation:** Paper identifies dimensions but does NOT establish numerical thresholds or quantitative measures for:
- *Country*-connection strength (how often? how meaningful?)
- TEK vitality (% of traditional knowledge transmitted per generation?)
- Caring-for-Country participation rates (benchmarks?)

**Recommendation for next research session:**
- Search for Indigenous wellbeing measurement tools with quantitative indicators
- Consult Mayi Kuwayu National Study for operationalized metrics
- Review Bhutan GNH Index methodology (may offer quantification approach)

### 5.2 Autocratization Mechanisms

**V-Dem 2025 describes trend but limited detail on:**
- What drives 45 countries to autocratize simultaneously? (contagion? economic crisis? AI surveillance enabling?)
- Reversibility rates (how often do autocracies democratize?)
- Tipping points (when does backsliding become irreversible?)

**Recommendation:** Deep dive into V-Dem methodology papers on autocratization waves, contagion effects, and recovery pathways.

### 5.3 AI Impact on Democracy (Emerging)

**V-Dem 2025 flags:** Freedom of expression declining in 44 countries, censorship increasing.

**Unexamined factor:** AI-enabled surveillance, social media manipulation, deepfakes, automated censorship.

**Recommendation:** Search for 2024-2025 research on AI's impact on democratic institutions (both threats and opportunities).
