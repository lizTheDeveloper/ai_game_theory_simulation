### On Cascades
The 2019 paper warns of **"domino-like cascades"** but provides **minimal quantitative detail** on timescales:

> "We argue that the intervention time left to prevent tipping could already have shrunk towards zero, whereas the reaction time to achieve net zero emissions is 30 years at best."

> "Even if the Paris Accord target of a 1.5 °C to 2.0 °C rise in temperature is met, we cannot exclude the risk that a cascade of feedbacks could push the Earth System irreversibly onto a 'Hothouse Earth' pathway."

**Key Quote on Timescales (Ice Sheets):**

### What It Does NOT Say
- ❌ NO mention of "50-100 year windows" for cascades
- ❌ NO quantitative cascade modeling results
- ❌ NO specific timeline for multi-element interactions
- ⚠️ This is a **commentary piece**, not a modeling study

## What Lenton et al. 2008 Actually Says

The **2008 PNAS paper** contains Table 1 with **individual element** transition timescales:

### Transition Timescales (Table 1)

| Tipping Element | Transition Time | Category |
|----------------|----------------|----------|
| Arctic summer sea ice | ≈10 years | Rapid |
| Sahara/Sahel monsoon | ≈10 years | Rapid |
| **Amazon rainforest** | **≈50 years** | **Gradual** |
| **Boreal forest** | **≈50 years** | **Gradual** |
| **Atlantic THC (AMOC)** | **≈100 years** | **Gradual** |
| **El Niño–Southern Oscillation** | **≈100 years** | **Gradual** |
| Greenland ice sheet | >300 years | Slow |
| West Antarctic ice sheet | >300 years | Slow |
| West Antarctic ice shelves | ≈100 years | Gradual |

**Critical Quote on Reversibility (NOT Cascades):**
> "A few of the changes associated with the feedbacks are **reversible on short timeframes of 50–100 years** (e.g., change in Arctic sea ice extent with a warming or cooling of the climate)."

### What This Means
The "50-100 year" timeframe in Lenton 2008 refers to:
1. **Individual element transitions** (Amazon, AMOC each taking 50-100 years)
2. **Reversibility windows** for some elements (NOT cascade dynamics)
3. **NOT the timeframe for cascading interactions** between elements

## Recent Research on Cascade Timescales (2022-2024)

### Armstrong McKay et al. 2022

**Full Citation:**
> Armstrong McKay, D. I., Staal, A., Abrams, J. F., Winkelmann, R., Sakschewski, B., Loriani, S., ... & Lenton, T. M. (2022). Exceeding 1.5° C global warming could trigger multiple climate tipping points. *Science*, 377(6611), eabn7950.

**Key Finding on Cascade Timescales:**
> "Transition timescales range from **decades to millennia**."

**Specific Examples:**
- Amazon rainforest: 50 years (10-500 years range)
- AMOC: 300 years
- Greenland Ice Sheet: 4,900 years (at 4°C warming)
- West Antarctic Ice Sheet: 2,400 years

**On Cascades:**
> "Multiple causal interactions between them with **risks of triggering cascades among CTPs** (climate tipping points), with their combined effect tending to lower temperature thresholds."

**NO specific "50-100 year" cascade window mentioned.**

---

### Wunderling et al. 2024 (Cascade Review)

**Full Citation:**
> Wunderling, N., von der Heydt, A. S., Aksenov, Y., Barker, S., Bastiaansen, R., Brovkin, V., ... & Winkelmann, R. (2024). Climate tipping point interactions and cascades: a review. *Earth System Dynamics*, 15(1), 41-74.

**DOI:** 10.5194/esd-15-41-2024

**Key Finding on Cascade Timescales:**
> "Tipping cascades cannot be ruled out on **centennial to millennial timescales** at global warming levels between 1.5 and 2.0 °C or on **shorter timescales if global warming surpassed 2.0 °C**."

**Fast vs. Slow Elements:**
- **Fast tipping elements** (months to decades): Amazon rainforest, AMOC
- **Slow tipping elements** (centuries to millennia): Greenland Ice Sheet

**Historical Analogue:**
> "Dansgaard–Oeschger events during the last ice age: repeated abrupt shifts occurred between cold and warm phases lasting **1,000–4,000 years**."

**Verdict:** Cascades unfold over **centennial to millennial** timescales, NOT 50-100 years.

---

### Wunderling et al. 2023 (Overshoot Study)

**Full Citation:**
> Wunderling, N., Winkelmann, R., Rockström, J., Loriani, S., Armstrong McKay, D. I., Ritchie, P. D., ... & Donges, J. F. (2023). Global warming overshoots increase risks of climate tipping cascades in a network model. *Nature Climate Change*, 13(1), 75-82.

**DOI:** 10.1038/s41558-022-01545-9

**Key Finding on Short-Term Risks:**
> "Some tipping risks are already realized **after 100–1,000 years**, with the AMOC and the Amazon rainforest showing strong dependence on peak temperature on these shorter time scales."

**Cascade Modeling:**
- Simulations run for 100, 1,000, and 50,000 years
- **100-year timescale:** Early warning signs, limited tipping
- **1,000-year timescale:** Cascades become significant
- Temperature overshoots increase cascade risk by **up to 72%**

**Verdict:** Cascades have observable effects after 100+ years, but full cascade unfolds over **centuries to millennia**.

- **NOT a 50-100 year cascade window**

## Simulation Implications

### Current Wiki Claim (Line 1147)
> "Peer-reviewed tipping cascade models (Lenton et al. 2019) suggest 50-100 year windows"

### Problems with This Claim

1. **Wrong Paper:** The 2019 paper is a commentary, not a tipping cascade model

3. **Contradicts Recent Research:** 2022-2024 literature shows cascades unfold over **centennial to millennial** timescales

   - **Threshold crossing:** Can happen in decades

   - **Cascade dynamics:** Centuries to millennia

- Amazon rainforest dieback: 50 years

- Boreal forest shift: 50 years

**For Tipping Cascades (2022-2024 literature):**

- **Medium-term (100-1,000 years):** Significant cascade effects emerge
- **Long-term (1,000+ years):** Full cascade unfolds (Dansgaard-Oeschger analogue: 1,000-4,000 years)
