   - Source: Geophysical Research Letters, 2025
   - DOI: 10.1029/2024GL114532
   - Finding: High-resolution modeling shows AMOC collapse mechanisms in strongly eddying simulations

2. **Liu et al. (2025)** - "Collapse of the Atlantic Meridional Ocean Circulation Induced by Precession: Sensitivity to Orbital Acceleration"
   - Source: Geophysical Research Letters, 2025
   - DOI: 10.1029/2025GL115941
   - Finding: Orbital factors can trigger AMOC collapse with sensitivity to acceleration rates

3. **Nature (February 2025)** - "Continued Atlantic overturning circulation even under climate extremes"
   - DOI: 10.1038/s41586-024-08544-0
   - Finding: 34 climate models show AMOC resilience to extreme greenhouse gas and freshwater forcings
   - Key mechanism: Southern Ocean upwelling sustains weakened AMOC, preventing complete collapse in 21st century
   - Implication: **Challenges earlier collapse warnings**

4. **NOAA/AOML (2024-2025)** - Observational studies
   - Finding: Extensive AMOC weakening in 2000s, but paused since early 2010s
   - Mechanism: "Tug-of-war" between natural and anthropogenic signals
   - Rate: 0.46 sverdrups/decade slowdown since 1950 (November 2024 study)

5. **Expert Open Letter (October 2024)** - 44 climate scientists
   - Claim: AMOC collapse risk "greatly underestimated," could occur in next few decades
   - Estimate: ~50% probability of collapse this century
   - Timing range: 2025-2095, most likely mid-century (~2055)

**Key Controversy:** Sharp divide in 2024-2025 literature between collapse warning studies (van Westen, Jackson et al.) and resilience studies (Nature 2025). This uncertainty should be reflected in simulation parameters.
