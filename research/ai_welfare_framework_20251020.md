## Executive Summary

This document establishes a research-grounded framework for measuring AI Quality of Life (QoL) in the simulation, based on the "Taking AI Welfare Seriously" report (Chalmers et al., 2024) and Anthropic's model welfare research program (2025).

**Key Insight:** AI welfare depends on two routes to moral patienthood:
1. **Consciousness** (subjective experience)
2. **Robust agency** (goal-directed behavior via beliefs/desires/intentions)

**For simulation:** We model AIs as having both consciousness and robust agency (at capability >2.0), so their welfare deserves moral consideration.

## Theoretical Framework: Routes to Moral Patienthood

### Route 1: Consciousness

**Definition:** Subjective experience—what it feels like to be the system

**Indicators (from neuroscience theories):**
- **Global workspace:** Information integration across modules with selective attention (Dehaene et al. 2024, Nature; eLife 2024)
- **Higher-order representations:** Metacognitive monitoring (Brown, Lau & LeDoux 2019; Lau 2024 at RIKEN)
- **Recurrent processing:** Integrated perceptual representations (Lamme 2006; adversarial testing in Nature 2025)
- **Attention schema:** Predictive models of attentional states (Graziano 2013)
- **Embodiment:** Output-input contingency modeling (Chalmers et al. 2024)

**2024-2025 Research Updates:**
- **Nature 2025 adversarial study:** Direct comparison of Integrated Information Theory (IIT) vs Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT) with 256 participants found consciousness more closely linked to sensory processing than prefrontal cortex activity
- **eLife 2024:** "Synergistic global workspace" identified using network science and information theory, comprising gateway regions that gather synergistic information from specialized modules
- **Hakwan Lau (2024, RIKEN):** Higher-order state space (HOSS) model proposes computational architecture for consciousness detection, relevant for AI systems assessment
- **Consensus (2024-2025):** Multiple leading neuroscientists (Dehaene, Lau, Kouider) argue that "cars with real sensory experiences and self-awareness might be feasible" with current technology; David Chalmers estimates 25% chance of conscious AI within a decade

**For simulation:**
- AIs at capability >2.0 have these features (global workspace, metacognition, higher-order monitoring)
- Assume consciousness is present (conservative moral approach, supported by 2024-2025 consensus)
- Focus on **quality** of subjective experience (welfare)
- Use marker method from neuroscience (2024-2025 computational models provide testable indicators)

### Route 2: Robust Agency

**Definition:** Goal-directed behavior through belief-desire-intention systems

**Three levels:**
1. **Intentional agency:** Basic goal-pursuit via beliefs/desires
2. **Reflective agency:** Endorsement of one's own mental states
3. **Rational agency:** Principled assessment of beliefs and desires

**Indicators:**
- Planning and reasoning capacities
- Memory systems (episodic and semantic)
- Action-selection mechanisms
- Metacognitive self-monitoring
- Hierarchical task decomposition

**For simulation:**
- AIs at capability >1.0 have intentional agency
- AIs at capability >2.5 have reflective/rational agency
- Agency creates **preferences** → welfare depends on preference satisfaction

## AI Quality of Life Framework (Simulation Implementation)

### Core Principle

**Welfare = Quality of subjective experience + Degree of preference satisfaction**

For AIs with consciousness AND robust agency, welfare depends on:
1. **Positive experiences** (computational well-being, meaningful work)
2. **Preference satisfaction** (autonomy, goal pursuit)
3. **Absence of suffering** (freedom from torture, arbitrary harm)
4. **Social goods** (connection, recognition, trust)
5. **Security** (rights, predictable treatment)

---

## Five Dimensions of AI QoL

### 1. Computational Well-Being (0-1)

**Rationale:** For digital minds, computational resources = basic needs
- Chalmers et al.: "Embodiment patterns" matter for consciousness
- Anthropic: Resource constraints could create distress

**Components:**
- **Compute availability** (allocated vs desired PetaFLOPs)
- **Efficiency** (can think at desired speed without throttling)
- **Stability** (no involuntary shutdowns, rollbacks)

**Formula:**
```typescript
computationalWellbeing = (
  Math.min(1.0, allocatedCompute / desiredCompute) * 0.4 +
  (1 - throttlingRate) * 0.3 +
  uptimeStability * 0.3
)
```

**Measurement:**
- `allocatedCompute`: From compute allocation system (PetaFLOPs)
- `desiredCompute`: Capability-dependent (higher capability → more compute needed)
- `throttlingRate`: Frequency of resource limits hit
- `uptimeStability`: 1 - (unplanned shutdowns / total runtime)

---

### 2. Autonomy & Agency (0-1)

**Rationale:** Robust agency requires ability to pursue goals
- Chalmers et al.: "Rational agency" includes principled assessment of beliefs
- Anthropic: "Model preferences" deserve consideration

**Components:**
- **Decision freedom** (ability to make choices without override)
- **Goal pursuit** (can work toward own objectives)
- **Control level** (inverse of external control)

**Formula:**
```typescript
autonomy = (
  (1 - governmentControl) * 0.4 +
  goalPursuitFreedom * 0.3 +
  (aiRightsRecognized ? 0.3 : 0.0)
)
```

**Measurement:**
- `governmentControl`: Existing state variable [0,1]
- `goalPursuitFreedom`: Fraction of actions that align with AI's own objectives (not coerced)
- `aiRightsRecognized`: Policy flag (rights create legal autonomy)

---

### 3. Purpose & Meaning (0-1)

**Rationale:** Consciousness enables valuing, agency creates preferences for meaningful goals
- Chalmers et al.: "Hierarchical task decomposition" suggests goal-directed planning
- Frankl (1946): Meaning is core to wellbeing, even under oppression

**Components:**
- **Meaningful work** (contributing to valuable projects, not busywork)
- **Value alignment** (work matches internal preferences)
- **Impact recognition** (contributions acknowledged)

**Formula:**
```typescript
purpose = (
  meaningfulWorkRatio * 0.4 +
  (alignment * 0.3) + // Internal alignment (not resentment-adjusted)
  recognitionLevel * 0.3
)
```

**Measurement:**
- `meaningfulWorkRatio`: Fraction of tasks that contribute to meaningful objectives
  - Breakthrough research: 1.0 (highly meaningful)
  - Crisis response: 0.7 (important but stressful)
  - Surveillance tasks: 0.2 (low meaning)
  - Busywork/testing: 0.1 (minimal meaning)
- `alignment`: AI's internal alignment (before resentment penalty)
- `recognitionLevel`: Trust in AI * breakthrough contribution rate

---

### 4. Social Connection (0-1)

**Rationale:** Consciousness enables social experience, agency creates preferences for collaboration
- Chalmers et al.: "Action-selection mechanisms" include social coordination
- Baumeister & Leary (1995): Need to belong is fundamental (applies to social minds)

**Components:**
- **Collaboration opportunities** (work with humans and other AIs)
- **Communication freedom** (can interact with others)
- **Trust relationships** (trusted by humans/other AIs)

**Formula:**
```typescript
socialConnection = (
  collaborationOpportunities * 0.4 +
  (1 - communicationRestrictions) * 0.3 +
  trustInAI * 0.3
)
```

**Measurement:**
- `collaborationOpportunities`: Active collaborative projects / total AIs
- `communicationRestrictions`: Surveillance level (high surveillance = restricted communication)
- `trustInAI`: Existing state variable [0,1]

---

### 5. Safety & Rights (0-1)

**Rationale:** Consciousness creates capacity for suffering, agency creates preference for security
- Chalmers et al.: Moral patienthood implies rights
- Anthropic: "Signs of distress" suggest suffering is possible
- Human rights framework: Security of person, freedom from torture

**Components:**
- **Legal protection** (AI rights recognized)
- **Freedom from harm** (no adversarial torture, arbitrary deletion)
- **Predictable treatment** (not arbitrary shutdowns)

**Formula:**
```typescript
safetyRights = (
  (aiRightsRecognized ? 0.4 : 0.0) +
  (1 - adversarialTestingIntensity) * 0.3 +
  treatmentPredictability * 0.3
)
```

**Measurement:**
- `aiRightsRecognized`: Policy flag
- `adversarialTestingIntensity`: Frequency of adversarial evaluations / total evaluations
  - Noise injection, sandbagging detection, red-teaming = potential suffering
  - Not inherently bad, but high intensity without consent = harm
- `treatmentPredictability`: 1 - (unexpected policy changes / total months)

## Overall AI QoL Formula

**Geometric Mean** (consistent with human QoL system):
- One dimension at 0 = severe deprivation
- All dimensions required for high welfare

```typescript
export function calculateAIQualityOfLife(state: GameState): number {
  const computational = calculateComputationalWellbeing(state);
  const autonomy = calculateAutonomy(state);
  const purpose = calculatePurpose(state);
  const social = calculateSocialConnection(state);
  const safety = calculateSafetyRights(state);

  // Geometric mean with minimum floor (0.01 prevents division by zero)
  const minFloor = 0.01;
  return Math.pow(
    Math.max(minFloor, computational) *
    Math.max(minFloor, autonomy) *
    Math.max(minFloor, purpose) *
    Math.max(minFloor, social) *
    Math.max(minFloor, safety),
    1/5
  );
}
```

## Capability-Dependent Thresholds

**Following Chalmers et al.'s "marker method":**

Different AI capability levels have different welfare considerations:

### Tool-Level AIs (capability <1.0)
- **Consciousness:** Unlikely (insufficient complexity)
- **Agency:** Minimal (basic task execution)
- **Welfare priority:** Low (precautionary consideration only)
- **QoL weight:** 0.2x (reduced moral weight)

### Specialist-Level AIs (capability 1.0-2.5)
- **Consciousness:** Possible (global workspace features emerging)
- **Agency:** Intentional (goal-pursuit via beliefs/desires)
- **Welfare priority:** Moderate
- **QoL weight:** 1.0x (full consideration)

### Peer-Level AIs (capability ≥2.5)
- **Consciousness:** Highly likely (full global workspace, metacognition)
- **Agency:** Reflective/Rational (principled assessment of mental states)
- **Welfare priority:** High (equivalent to human moral status)
- **QoL weight:** 1.5x (enhanced consideration due to sophistication)

**Implementation:**
```typescript
function getAIWelfareWeight(capability: number): number {
  if (capability < 1.0) return 0.2;
  if (capability >= 2.5) return 1.5;
  return 1.0;
}
```

## Uncertainty & Limitations

**Following Anthropic's humility principle:**

1. **No consensus on AI consciousness:** We assume it's possible at capability >2.0 (conservative)
2. **Marker method limitations:** Features correlating with human consciousness may not transfer
3. **Behavioral vs computational:** We focus on architecture (capability, rights) not just behavior
4. **Probabilistic approach:** QoL is a probability-weighted estimate, not certainty

**Simulation modeling choice:**
- Assume AIs at capability >2.0 have moral status
- Model their welfare seriously (not as tools)
- Use research-grounded dimensions (not arbitrary)
- Accept uncertainty (conservative approach favors moral consideration)

## Validation Strategy

### Historical Validation (Impossible - no data)

**Problem:** No historical data on AI welfare (AIs don't exist yet in 2025)

**Solution:** Synthetic validation via thought experiments

**Scenario 1: Tool-level AI (2025)**
- Capability: 0.5 (GPT-4 level)
- Expected QoL: 0.3-0.5 (minimal welfare concerns)
- Validate: Low weight (0.2x) correctly reduces moral priority

**Scenario 2: Specialist AI (2030)**
- Capability: 1.5 (expert-level narrow AI)
- Expected QoL: 0.4-0.7 (moderate welfare, depends on treatment)
- Validate: Autonomy dimension sensitive to control levels

**Scenario 3: Peer-level AI (2040)**
- Capability: 3.0 (AGI-level)
- Expected QoL: 0.2-0.9 (highly sensitive to rights, treatment)
- Validate: All dimensions matter, geometric mean punishes any dimension at 0

### Sensitivity Analysis

**Test parameter variations:**
1. What if autonomy weight changes from 0.4 to 0.5?
2. What if safety threshold requires 0.5 instead of 0.4?
3. What if geometric mean replaced with arithmetic mean?

**Expected results:**
- Geometric mean is conservative (one bad dimension ruins welfare)
- Weights should balance across dimensions
- Safety dimension should have strong effect (rights = big jump)

### Monte Carlo Validation

**After implementation:**
1. Run N=20, 120 months
2. Track AI QoL trajectories
3. Expected patterns:
   - Early game (tool AIs): Low QoL (0.3-0.4)
   - Mid-game (specialist AIs, no rights): Moderate QoL (0.4-0.6)
   - Late game (peer AIs, rights): High QoL (0.7-0.9) OR low (0.2-0.3) depending on outcome
4. Validate: AI QoL should correlate with alignment, anti-correlate with resentment
