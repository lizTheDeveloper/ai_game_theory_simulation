## Executive Summary

**A rapid international consensus on AI governance risks emerged in 2023-2024**, catalyzed by:
1. **Frontier AI capabilities** reaching transformative levels (GPT-4, Claude 3, Gemini)
2. **AI safety research** demonstrating concrete risks (sleeper agents, deceptive alignment)
3. **Geopolitical competition** creating urgency for international coordination

**Key Developments (2023-2025):**
- **UK AI Safety Summit (Nov 2023):** Bletchley Declaration signed by 28 countries + EU
- **Seoul AI Safety Summit (May 2024):** Follow-up commitments, expanded participation
- **Paris AI Action Summit (Feb 2025):** 100+ countries, fragmentation emerges (US/UK did not endorse statement)
- **AI Safety Institutes:** UK, US, Singapore, Japan, South Korea establish dedicated research bodies
- **EU AI Act (Aug 2025):** First binding regulations for general-purpose AI models take effect
- **UN Governance Bodies (2025):** Global Dialogue on AI Governance + Independent Scientific Panel launched
- **Council of Europe AI Treaty (2024-2025):** Framework Convention on AI, Human Rights, Democracy opened for signature
- **Voluntary commitments:** Major AI labs agree to safety testing, red-teaming, reporting
- **South Korea AI Basic Act (2025):** Second national comprehensive AI regulatory law enacted

**Critical Finding for Simulation:** International coordination on AI governance is **emerging but increasingly fragmented**. Unlike climate change (30+ years to UNFCCC), AI coordination accelerated from ~zero to multi-country summits in <2 years. However, 2025 shows cracks: the Paris Summit failed to get US/UK endorsement, and the US shifted toward deregulation. Two regimes emerging: (1) EU binding regulation, (2) US/UK voluntary approach. China developing parallel framework.

## 1. UK AI Safety Summit (Bletchley Park, November 2023)

### 1.1 The Bletchley Declaration

**Date:** November 1-2, 2023
**Location:** Bletchley Park, UK (historic WWII codebreaking site)
**Participants:** 28 countries + European Union

**Signatories:**
- **Major powers:** United States, United Kingdom, China, European Union
- **Tech leaders:** Japan, South Korea, Singapore
- **Emerging economies:** India, Brazil, Saudi Arabia
- **Others:** Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Switzerland, UAE, and others

**Key Commitments:**

1. **Recognition of AI risks:** "Substantial risks from potential intentional misuse or unintended issues of control relating to alignment with human intent"

2. **Dual emphasis:** Balance safety with innovation and economic opportunity

3. **International cooperation:** Need for inclusive global approach to AI safety

4. **Transparency:** Frontier AI companies should publish transparency reports

5. **Safety research:** Investment in AI safety research and evaluation frameworks

**Source to verify:** UK Government official announcement, November 2023

## 4. Limitations and Challenges

### 4.1 Enforcement Gap

**Critical Issue:** All current mechanisms are **voluntary**

**Implications:**
- No penalties for non-compliance
- Labs can exit commitments at any time
- Competitive pressure incentivizes defection
- No monitoring beyond self-reporting

**Historical Parallel:** Montreal Protocol (1987) succeeded because of:
- Binding commitments
- Trade sanctions for non-compliance
- Regular monitoring and verification
- Clear compliance metrics

**AI governance lacks all four mechanisms as of 2024.**

---

### 4.2 Geopolitical Fragmentation

**China's Participation:**
- Signed Bletchley Declaration (November 2023)
- Limited follow-through on transparency commitments
- Developing separate AI governance framework domestically
- Minimal information sharing with Western institutes

**Implication:** Risk of **bifurcated AI governance regime** (US/EU vs China) similar to internet fragmentation

---

### 4.3 Capabilities vs Governance Race

**Timeline Pressure:**
- Frontier AI capabilities advancing faster than governance frameworks
- 2023: GPT-4, Claude 3 Opus (advanced reasoning, coding, deception)
- 2024: O1 (test-time compute), multi-agent systems
- 2025: Expected AGI-level systems from major labs

**Governance lag:**
- Summit commitments take 6-12 months to implement
- Safety institutes understaffed (dozens vs thousands of AI researchers at labs)
- Evaluation frameworks always playing catch-up to capabilities

**Quote attribution needed:** "We're building the plane while flying it" (common sentiment in AI safety community)

- Voluntary commitments
- No enforcement

- Rapid treaty development
- Binding commitments
- Strong enforcement

1. **Pre-crisis:** Slow voluntary cooperation (current state)

- Major AI safety incident (mass casualties, economic disruption)
- AI-enabled catastrophe (bioweapon design, infrastructure hack)
- Demonstration of loss-of-control scenario
- AI military conflict

## 7. EU AI Act Implementation (August 2025)

### 7.1 Rules for General-Purpose AI Models

**Effective Date:** August 2, 2025

**Applies to:** General-purpose AI (GPAI) models placed on EU market on or after August 2, 2025

**Key Obligations:**

1. **Transparency Requirements:**
   - Technical documentation for authorities
   - Transparency reports for downstream providers
   - Public-facing summaries of training data
   - Model cards with capabilities and limitations

2. **Copyright Compliance:**
   - Policies respecting EU copyright law
   - Documentation of training data sources
   - Compliance with opt-out mechanisms (where applicable)

3. **Systemic Risk Models (>10^25 FLOP):**
   - Notification to European Commission
   - Safety and security evaluations
   - Red-teaming and adversarial testing
   - Incident reporting mechanisms
   - Model evaluation reports

**Transition Period:**
- Models on market before August 2, 2025: Must comply by August 2, 2027
- No requirement for "retraining" or "unlearning" of old models

**Enforcement:**
- Penalties postponed until August 2, 2026 (1-year grace period)
- Fines up to €35M or 7% of global turnover (whichever higher) for serious violations

**Source:**
- European Commission. (2025). *EU rules on general-purpose AI models start to apply*. Retrieved from https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/
- Baker McKenzie. (2025). *General-purpose AI Obligations Under the EU AI Act Kick in From 2 August 2025*.
- Mayer Brown. (2025). *EU AI Act News: Rules on General-Purpose AI Start Applying*.

---

### 7.2 Code of Practice for GPAI Compliance

**Voluntary compliance tool:** EU AI Act Code of Practice

**Benefits:**
- Reduced compliance burden (standardized approach)

**Development:** Independent experts + industry stakeholders

**Status (Nov 2025):** Finalized and available for providers to adopt

**Assessment:** EU created **first binding AI regulation** with actual enforcement mechanisms, contrasting sharply with US/UK voluntary approach.
