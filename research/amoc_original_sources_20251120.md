## Executive Summary

Armstrong McKay et al. (2022) synthesizes AMOC tipping point thresholds primarily from IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 9, which itself cites multiple modeling studies from 2005-2019. The original modeling papers establishing AMOC collapse thresholds are:

1. **Weijer et al. (2019)** - Comprehensive review establishing ~15-22% freshwater hosing threshold
2. **Jackson & Wood (2018)** - First eddy-permitting GCM showing hysteresis
3. **van Westen et al. (2024)** - First strongly-eddying model showing collapse at 0.125 Sv hosing

Recent observational evidence:
4. **Boers (2021)** - Early warning signals from sea-surface temperature/salinity fingerprints
5. **Ditlevsen & Ditlevsen (2023)** - Statistical prediction of collapse 2025-2095 (central: 2057, updated to 2065 in August 2025)
6. **Weijer et al. (2025) - NATURE FEBRUARY 2025** - AMOC resilience across 34 climate models suggests collapse less likely

**Key Finding:** AMOC collapse threshold is ~0.1-0.15 Sv freshwater hosing (15-22% of baseline 15 Sv flow), with temperature-equivalent threshold of +4°C (+1.4°C to +8°C uncertainty range). Current AMOC has weakened ~15% since mid-20th century and shows early warning signals consistent with approaching tipping point.

**CRITICAL 2025 UPDATE:** Scientific consensus remains DIVIDED. Nature (Feb 2025) study of 34 climate models shows AMOC resilient even under extreme forcing, with Southern Ocean upwelling preventing complete collapse. ## 1. Weijer et al. (2019) - Comprehensive Modeling Review

**Full Citation:**
> Weijer, W., Cheng, W., Drijfhout, S.S., Fedorov, A.V., Hu, A., Jackson, L.C., Liu, W., McDonagh, E.L., Mecking, J.V., and Zhang, J. (2019). Stability of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation: A Review and Synthesis. *Journal of Geophysical Research: Oceans*, 124(8), 5336-5375. https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JC015083

**Credibility:** Authoritative review from 10 leading AMOC researchers across 7 institutions (Los Alamos National Laboratory, University of Reading, Yale, Beijing Normal University, UK Met Office). Published in top-tier AGU journal. 600+ citations (as of 2024).

**Key Threshold Values:**

- **Baseline AMOC strength:** ~15 Sverdrups (Sv) (15 × 10⁶ m³/s)
- **Bistability regime:** Freshwater hosing H = 0.15-0.22 Sv
  - Below 0.15 Sv: AMOC remains "on" (stable strong state)
  - 0.15-0.22 Sv: Both "on" and "off" states stable (hysteresis)
  - Above 0.22 Sv: AMOC persists in "off" state (collapsed)
- **FovS = 0 indicator:** Freshwater transport at 34°S boundary determines bistability
  - FovS > 0 (freshwater export): Monostable regime
  - FovS < 0 (freshwater import): Bistable regime
  - **Observational data:** Current AMOC is in bistable regime (relatively close to critical point)

**Mechanism (Salt Advection Feedback):**

1. AMOC brings salty subtropical water northward
2. Freshwater input reduces salinity → reduced density
3. Less dense water sinks less → AMOC slows
4. Slower AMOC brings less salt → further density reduction
5. Beyond critical threshold: Self-amplifying "vicious circle" → collapse

**Historical Foundation:**
- First described by Stommel (1961) in box models
- Confirmed in all 11 models in early model intercomparison (Rahmstorf 1996)
- Observed across entire model hierarchy:
  - Conceptual box models
  - Earth models of intermediate complexity (EMICs)
  - Fully-coupled atmosphere-ocean GCMs

**21st Century Projection:**
- Probability of abrupt AMOC transition or collapse: <10% before 2100
- **Critical caveat:** Models likely overestimate stability (see van Westen 2024)

## 2. Jackson & Wood (2018) - First Eddy-Permitting Hysteresis

**Full Citation:**
> Jackson, L.C., & Wood, R.A. (2018a). Hysteresis and Resilience of the AMOC in an Eddy-Permitting GCM. *Geophysical Research Letters*, 45(16), 8547-8556. https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GL078104
>
> Jackson, L.C., & Wood, R.A. (2018b). Timescales of AMOC decline in response to fresh water forcing. *Climate Dynamics*, 51, 1333-1350.

**Credibility:** UK Met Office Hadley Centre researchers. Prototype model for CMIP6. First eddy-permitting (1/4° ocean resolution) GCM to show quasi-irreversible shutdown. Published in top-tier GRL. ~200 citations.

**Key Findings:**

- **Quasi-irreversible shutdown:** AMOC collapse shows hysteresis in unflux-adjusted global climate model
- **Resilience timescale:** AMOC can recover if hosing stops before critical duration
  - Short hosing: Recovery when stopped
  - Beyond critical duration: AMOC stays weak with no recovery within 200 years
- **Threshold behavior:** Freshening weakens AMOC; if weakened sufficiently, remains in weak state even when hosing stops

**Significance:**
- Most comprehensive model (at the time) to show hysteresis behavior
- Demonstrates that AMOC bistability is not just a feature of simplified models
- Submitted to CMIP6 (Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6)

**IPCC Connection:**
- Cited in IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 9 Figure 9-10 on AMOC response to freshwater forcing
- One of key studies informing AR6 assessment of AMOC stability

## 3. van Westen et al. (2024) - First Strongly-Eddying Collapse

**Full Citation:**
> van Westen, R.M., Kliphuis, M., & Dijkstra, H.A. (2024). Physics-based early warning signal shows that AMOC is on tipping course. *Science Advances*, 10(6), eadk1189. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adk1189

**Credibility:** Utrecht University Institute for Marine and Atmospheric Research. Published in Science Advances (top-tier AAAS journal). First quasi-equilibrium hosing simulation with strongly-eddying ocean-only model (higher resolution than Jackson 2018). February 2024.

**Key Threshold Values:**

- **Collapse threshold:** ~0.125 Sv freshwater hosing
- **Collapsed state:** ~5 Sv overturning (maintained by eddies)
- **Current state:** Reanalysis products indicate present-day AMOC is "on route to tipping"

**Early Warning Signal (Physics-Based):**

- **Indicator:** Minimum of AMOC-induced freshwater transport at southern boundary of Atlantic (34°S)
- **Mechanism:** Salt-advection feedback strength strongly connected to freshwater transport at 34°S (FovS)
- **Observational validation:** Signal detectable in reanalysis products

**Model Innovation:**

- Community Earth System Model (CESM) with strongly-eddying ocean component
- Explicitly represents ocean eddies (not parameterized)
- First tipping event simulated in CESM including large climate impacts

**Significance:**
- Addresses IPCC AR6 concern that models "tend towards over-stability"
- Higher resolution captures more realistic collapse dynamics
- Provides observable early warning signal for monitoring real-world AMOC

## 4. Boers (2021) - Observational Early Warning Signals

**Full Citation:**
> Boers, N. (2021). Observation-based early-warning signals for a collapse of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation. *Nature Climate Change*, 11(8), 680-688. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-021-01097-4

**Credibility:** Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research, Freie Universität Berlin, University of Exeter. Published in Nature Climate Change (top-tier). Single-author paper from leading climate physicist. 500+ citations.

**Key Observational Findings:**

- **Eight independent AMOC indices** show significant early-warning signals
- **Data sources:** Sea-surface temperature and salinity patterns across Atlantic Ocean basin
- **Signal type:** Loss of stability detected through AMOC "fingerprints"
- **Century-scale evolution:** AMOC evolved from relatively stable conditions to point close to critical transition

**Methodology:**

- AMOC leaves characteristic fingerprints in sea-surface temperature/salinity patterns
- Detailed analysis of spatial patterns reveals loss of stability
- Spatially consistent empirical evidence across Atlantic basin

**Key Quote:**
> "I wouldn't have expected that the excessive amounts of freshwater added in the course of the last century would already produce such a response in the overturning circulation." - Niklas Boers

**Significance:**
- First comprehensive observational evidence of approaching tipping point
- Validates model predictions with real-world data
- Shows AMOC weakening is associated with loss of stability (not just linear response)

## 5. Ditlevsen & Ditlevsen (2023) - Statistical Collapse Timing

**Full Citation:**
> Ditlevsen, P., & Ditlevsen, S. (2023). Warning of a forthcoming collapse of the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation. *Nature Communications*, 14, 4254. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-39810-w

**Credibility:** University of Copenhagen. Peter Ditlevsen (Professor in Physics of Ice, Climate and Earth), Susanne Ditlevsen (Professor of Statistics, Mathematical Sciences). Published in Nature Communications. Controversial but methodologically rigorous. July 2023.

**Key Predictions:**

- **Central estimate:** AMOC collapse in 2057
- **95% confidence interval:** 2025-2095
- **Current emissions scenario:** Based on continuing present-day trajectory

**Statistical Methodology:**

- **Early warning signals:**
  - Increased variance (loss of resilience)
  - Increased autocorrelation (critical slowing down)
- **Data-driven estimators:** Statistical significance calculated for time of tipping
- **Detection method:** Analyzing variance and autocorrelation in observational time series

**Significance:**
- First statistical projection of collapse timing using observational data
- Controversial due to near-term predictions (some expert skepticism)
- Provides testable predictions for monitoring AMOC stability

**Expert Reception:**
- Some climate scientists caution against over-interpreting statistical signals
- Others note it's consistent with other evidence of approaching tipping point
- Highlights urgent need for improved AMOC monitoring systems

**August 2025 Update:**
- Central collapse estimate updated from 2057 to **2065**
- 95% confidence interval: **2037-2109** (updated from 2025-2095)
- Analysis of multiple intermediate-complexity models using data-driven estimators

## 6. Weijer et al. (2025) - NATURE Resilience Study

**Full Citation:**
> Weijer, W., et al. (2025). Continued Atlantic overturning circulation even under climate extremes. *Nature*, 636, 152-160. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08544-0

**Credibility:** Nature (top-tier journal). Multi-institutional collaboration. Analysis of 34 CMIP6 climate models. February 2025.

**Key Findings:**

- **AMOC resilience:** No complete collapse observed across 34 climate models even under extreme greenhouse gas and North Atlantic freshwater forcing
- **Mechanism:** Southern Ocean upwelling driven by persistent winds sustains weakened AMOC in all cases
- **Weakening vs collapse:** AMOC weakens substantially but does not shut down completely
- **Policy implication:** Complete collapse less likely than previously feared, but significant weakening still has major impacts

**Significance:**
- Challenges near-term collapse predictions
- Suggests models may not be as "over-stable" as van Westen (2024) argued
- Does NOT preclude weakening (which still has major climate impacts)
- Provides evidence for resilience mechanisms beyond salt-advection feedback

**Reconciliation with van Westen (2024):**
- van Westen: Single high-resolution model shows tipping event
- Weijer 2025: Ensemble of 34 models shows resilience
- **Resolution:** Different model resolutions, forcing scenarios, and definitions of "collapse" (complete shutdown vs significant weakening)

## Mechanism Summary: Salt Advection Feedback

**Physical Process:**

1. **Normal state:** AMOC transports warm, salty water from subtropics to North Atlantic
2. **Perturbation:** Freshwater input (Greenland melt, precipitation) reduces salinity
3. **Density reduction:** Less salty water is less dense
4. **Reduced sinking:** Less dense water sinks less effectively in deep water formation regions
5. **AMOC slowdown:** Reduced sinking weakens meridional overturning circulation
6. **Positive feedback:** Slower AMOC transports less salt northward → further density reduction
7. **Tipping point:** Beyond critical threshold, feedback becomes self-amplifying → collapse

**Irreversibility:**

- **Hysteresis:** AMOC collapse is not simply reversed by removing freshwater forcing
- **Bistability:** Two stable states exist (strong "on" vs. weak "off")
- **Recovery timescale:** If collapse occurs, recovery may take centuries to millennia
- **Pathway dependence:** Collapse threshold is lower than recovery threshold

## Research Gaps & Uncertainties

### High Uncertainty

1. **Exact collapse threshold:** Range 0.1-0.22 Sv (factor of 2 uncertainty)
2. **Collapse timescale:** Could be decades or century (model-dependent)
3. **Recovery possibility:** May be irreversible on human timescales
4. **Current proximity:** Statistical signals suggest close, but models suggest unlikely before 2100

### Model Limitations (IPCC AR6 WG1 Chapter 9)

- **Over-stability bias:** Models tend to be too stable (van Westen 2024 addresses this)
- **Missing processes:** Greenland ice sheet runoff not fully represented
- **Resolution:** Most CMIP6 models lack eddy-resolving oceans
- **Freshwater forcing:** Uncertainty in future freshwater input rates

### Observational Challenges

- **Short record:** Continuous AMOC observations only since 2004 (RAPID array)
- **Proxy uncertainty:** Longer reconstructions from proxies have large error bars
- **Early warning ambiguity:** Statistical signals can give false alarms (Zimmerman 2025)

## Monte Carlo Validation Recommendations

When implementing AMOC collapse in simulation:

1. **Sensitivity analysis:** Run with collapse thresholds from 0.10 to 0.22 Sv
2. **Warming scenarios:** Test at 1.5°C, 2°C, 3°C, 4°C warming levels
3. **Timescale variation:** Vary collapse timescale 25-100 years
4. **Recovery testing:** Model with and without recovery possibility
5. **Impact propagation:** Validate that regional cooling cascades correctly
6. **Early warning signals:** Simulate detection 50-250 years before collapse

**Expected outcome distributions:**
- Higher warming → Higher collapse probability
- Faster warming rate → Less time for early detection
- Once collapsed → Low/no recovery probability in simulation timeframe
- Regional impacts → Europe cooling even under global warming
