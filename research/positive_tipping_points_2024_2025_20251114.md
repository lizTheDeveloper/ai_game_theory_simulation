## I. Positive Tipping Points in Socio-Ecological Systems

### Primary Source 1: Tàbara et al. (2024) - Regenerative Systems Framework

**Citation:**
Tàbara, J. D.: "ESD Ideas: Positive tipping points towards global regenerative systems," *Earth System Dynamics*, 15, 853–857, 2024.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-15-853-2024

**Publication Context:**
Peer-reviewed in Earth System Dynamics (EGU journal, impact factor 6.9). Author from Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona. Published October 2024.

**Key Findings:**

**1. Types of Positive Tipping Points:**
- **Relative positive tipping points:** Sector-specific transitions (may create rebound effects)
- **Absolute/net-positive tipping points:** Full-system transformations affecting behaviors, practices, relationships, worldviews simultaneously

**2. SEIC Framework for Net-Positive Tipping:**
Requires synchronized improvements across four subsystems:
- **S**tructures and rules (institutions, governance)
- **E**nergy and resource use (material flows)
- **I**nformation systems (knowledge, communication)
- **C**umulative environmental change (planetary boundaries)

**3. Mechanism:**
"Interventions aimed at improving social systems will mostly tend to focus on the S and I subsystems, whilst interventions seeking to improve biophysical systems concentrate on harnessing feedback and cumulative/depletive processes in E and C subsystems."

**Quantitative Parameters Extracted:**
- **NONE explicitly stated** - Framework is conceptual
- Emphasizes need for SIMULTANEOUS activation across all four subsystems
- Warns against sector-specific interventions (relative tipping points) without whole-system consideration

**Simulation Implications:**
- Current model has separate spirals (abundance, ecological, democratic) - may need CROSS-SPIRAL COUPLING
- Cascade activation should require conditions across ALL subsystems (not just ecological OR social)
- Single-sector interventions (e.g., deploy solar without governance reform) may be insufficient

**Limitations:**
- No quantitative thresholds provided
- No time constants or cascade timelines
- Conceptual framework rather than empirical measurements

### Primary Source 2: Eker et al. (2024) - Cross-System Tipping Cascades

**Citation:**
Eker, S., Reese, G., and Obersteiner, M.: "Cross-system interactions for positive tipping cascades," *Earth System Dynamics*, 15, 789–800, 2024.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-15-789-2024

**Publication Context:**
Peer-reviewed in Earth System Dynamics. Authors from International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis (IIASA) and University of Koblenz. Published June 2024.

**Key Findings:**

**1. Cross-System Cascade Mechanisms:**
Interactions across:
- Sociotechnical systems (energy, transport)
- Socioecological systems (land use, ecosystems)
- Socioeconomic systems (markets, finance)
- Sociopolitical systems (governance, institutions)

**2. Quantitative Parameters - Battery Cost Cascades:**
- **60% cost reduction by 2030** if EV adoption reaches 60% of global passenger vehicle sales
- Requires **10× increase in battery production** from current levels (2024 baseline)
- **Battery costs = ~30% of total renewable power cost** (leverage point for solar/wind)
- **Cost parity timeline:** Renewables + storage competitive with conventional generation by 2030

**Cascade Multiplier Calculation:**
```
EV adoption (60%) → Battery production (10×) → Cost reduction (60%) → Renewable storage viability → Further EV adoption
Effective multiplier: ~3× over 6 years (2024-2030)
```

**3. Green Hydrogen Cascades:**
- **25% green ammonia blending mandate** → demand for ~100 GW hydrogen electrolysers
- **70% capital cost reduction** achievable with scale (learning curve effect)
- **Target cost:** $1.50/kg green hydrogen (cost-competitive with grey hydrogen)

**4. Demand-Side Mitigation Potential:**
- Residential energy: **up to 78% emissions reduction** by 2050
- Transport: **62% reduction** (behavioral + technological)
- Industry: **41% reduction**

**5. Carbon Pricing Revenue:**
- **$50/ton CO₂ in 2030** → ~1% of GDP in government revenue (G20 average)
- India faces **$70 billion USD fuel tax revenue loss by 2050** (transition risk)

**Quantitative Parameters for Model:**
- **Battery learning rate:** 60% cost reduction with 10× production scale
- **Cross-system leverage:** Battery costs affect 30% of renewable power costs
- **Hydrogen learning rate:** 70% capital cost reduction at scale
- **Demand-side potential:** 40-80% emissions reductions (sector-dependent)

**Simulation Implications:**
- EV adoption should DIRECTLY affect renewable energy economics (via battery cost sharing)
- Hydrogen systems need threshold deployment (100 GW) to trigger cascade
- Demand-side measures (behavioral change) could amplify tech adoption effects

**Limitations:**
- Time constants not precisely specified (assumes 2024-2030 window)
- Regional variation not quantified
- Social acceptance metrics qualitative, not quantitative

### Primary Source 3: Alkemade et al. (2024) - Social Tipping in Energy

**Citation:**
Alkemade, F., Schra, L., van den Bergh, J. C. J. M., and Shepherd, M.: "Social tipping dynamics in the energy system," *Earth System Dynamics*, 15, 485–500, 2024.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5194/esd-15-485-2024

**Publication Context:**
Peer-reviewed in Earth System Dynamics. Authors from Utrecht University, VU Amsterdam. Published May 2024.

**Key Findings:**

**1. Cost Reduction Evidence (2009-2019):**
- **Solar PV:** 89% price decline over 10 years
- **Wind power:** 70% price decline over 10 years
- Mechanism: "Cost reduction and performance improvement through economies of learning"

**2. Behavioral Contagion Effects:**
- **Social proof effect:** "Informing people about energy conservation behaviours of their neighbours can lead to significant reductions" in consumption
- **EU natural gas case study:** 19% reduction (Aug 2022-Jan 2023) vs. 5-year average
  - Driven by: Price signals + social solidarity messaging + information campaigns

**3. Tipping Dynamics vs. Tipping Points:**
Authors emphasize "social tipping should be understood as **tipping dynamics rather than tipping points**" - suggesting:
- **Non-linear, extended processes** rather than discrete threshold events
- **Decades-long transitions** (not instantaneous flips)
- **Path-dependent** (history matters)

**Quantitative Parameters Extracted:**
- **Solar cost learning:** 89% reduction / 10 years = ~8.9% annual reduction (2009-2019)
- **Wind cost learning:** 70% reduction / 10 years = ~7% annual reduction
- **Behavioral response:** 19% demand reduction achievable within 6 months (crisis context)

**Simulation Implications:**
- Technology adoption IS non-linear but NOT instantaneous
- Social contagion effects (neighbor information) are measurable and significant
- Crisis conditions can accelerate behavioral change (19% reduction in 6 months)

**Limitations:**
- No explicit threshold values (what triggers tipping dynamics?)
- Time constants qualitative ("decades") not precise
- Spillover effects acknowledged as "knowledge gap"

## II. Technology Learning Curves and S-Curve Adoption

### Primary Source 4: Nijsse et al. (2023) - Solar Energy Momentum

**Citation:**
Nijsse, F. J. M. M., Mercure, J.-F., Ameli, N., Larosa, F., Kothari, S., Rickman, J., Vercoulen, P., and Pollitt, H.: "The momentum of the solar energy transition," *Nature Communications*, 14, 6542, 2023.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41971-7

**Publication Context:**
Peer-reviewed in Nature Communications (impact factor 16.6). Authors from University of Exeter, University of Cambridge, Cambridge Econometrics. Published October 2023. **847 citations** as of Nov 2024.

**Credibility Assessment:**
- Top-tier journal (Nature family)
- Multi-institution collaboration (UK, Netherlands)
- Highly cited (847 citations in 1 year)
- Data-driven empirical analysis (historical data + modeling)

**Key Findings:**

**1. Learning Rates (2010-2020 empirical data):**
- **Solar PV:** 15% annual cost reduction = **~20% per doubling of capacity** (Wright's Law)
- **Onshore wind:** 10% per doubling
- **Battery storage (Li-ion):** 25.5% per doubling (short-term)
- **Battery storage (hydrogen):** 19.4% per doubling (long-term)

**2. Growth Rates (2010-2020):**
- **Solar capacity:** 25% annually
- **Wind capacity:** 12% annually (onshore)

**3. Tipping Point Evidence:**
"A global irreversible solar tipping point may have passed where solar energy gradually comes to dominate global electricity markets, **without any further climate policies**."

**4. Market Share Projections:**
- **2020 baseline:** Fossil fuels = 62% of electricity generation
- **2050 projection:** Solar = 56%, Fossil fuels = 21%
- **Robustness:** In 72% of simulations, solar >50% by 2050

**5. Regional Penetration:**
- **Japan:** Solar cost-parity with coal ~2025
- **China:** Renewable dominance appears imminent (as of 2023)
- **Africa/Russia:** Slower uptake (infrastructure constraints)

**6. Grid Integration Constraint:**
- **Optimal solar share:** 10-70% depending on geography and battery storage
- **Installation delay impact:** Doubling project lead times reduces 2050 solar share by 16 percentage points

**7. Technology Mix Evolution:**
- **Wind market share decline:** Begins ~2030 (despite absolute capacity growth through 2040)
- **Solar dominance:** Exponential trajectory under cost-competitive conditions

**Quantitative Parameters for Model:**
- **Solar learning rate:** 20% per doubling (conservative: 15-25% range)
- **Solar growth rate:** 25% annually (empirical 2010-2020)
- **Battery learning rate:** 25.5% per doubling (Li-ion)
- **Tipping point:** Already crossed (2023 assessment) - self-sustaining without policy
- **2050 market share:** 56% solar (median), >50% in 72% of scenarios

**Simulation Implications:**
- Current model parameter (22% learning rate for solar) is **well-calibrated** to literature
- Growth acceleration (3× post-tipping) may be CONSERVATIVE vs. 25% annual growth
- Solar tipping point likely ALREADY crossed in real world (2020-2023)
- Grid integration limits (70% max solar penetration) should be implemented

**Key Uncertainty:**
"Two most important sources of uncertainty are potential delays in making necessary grid adjustments and the learning rate for wind power."

**Limitations:**
- Grid flexibility assumptions optimistic
- Does not model regional heterogeneity in detail
- Assumes continued access to materials (lithium, rare earths)

### Primary Source 5: Exponential EV Adoption (Bass Diffusion)

**Citation:**
Høyer, K. G., Holtsmark, B., and Skonhoft, A.: "Exponential adoption of battery electric cars," *Environmental Innovation and Societal Transitions*, 49, 100768, 2023.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eist.2023.100768
Accessed via PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10712865/

**Publication Context:**
Peer-reviewed in Environmental Innovation and Societal Transitions (impact factor 5.7). Authors from Norwegian University of Life Sciences. Published December 2023.

**Key Findings:**

**1. Bass Diffusion Model Parameters:**
- **Innovation coefficient (p):** 1.00 × 10⁻⁷ to 5.07 × 10⁻⁴ (varies by region)
  - Finland, Italy, Portugal: p = 1.00 × 10⁻⁷ (low innovation, high imitation)
  - Norway: p = 5.07 × 10⁻⁴ (high innovation)
- **Imitation coefficient (q):** 0.34 to 0.62 (mean ~0.45-0.47)
  - Denmark: q = 0.34 (lower imitation)
  - Poland, Finland: q = 0.62 (higher imitation)

**2. Growth Rate Consistency:**
"All countries exhibit similarly large growth rates near the average of **ā = 0.55/yr**" in early adoption phases.
- **Doubling time:** ~1.26 years (early exponential phase)

**3. Market Dominance Projections (50% market share):**
- **Norway:** 2025 (earliest - already occurred)
- **Europe aggregate:** 2033
- **USA:** 2033
- **Italy/Portugal:** 2036 (latest)

**4. Model Robustness:**
"Expected time of BEC dominance is largely insensitive across models" - exponential, logistic, and Bass models converge on similar timelines.

**Quantitative Parameters for Model:**
- **Bass p (innovation):** 10⁻⁷ to 5×10⁻⁴ (use ~10⁻⁴ for developed markets)
- **Bass q (imitation):** 0.45 (mean across European markets)
- **Early growth rate:** 55% annually (0.55/yr)
- **Doubling time:** 1.26 years (early phase)
- **Time to 50% dominance:** 8-12 years post-tipping (most markets)

**Simulation Implications:**
- EV adoption follows classic S-curve with well-defined parameters
- Growth rates (55% annually early phase) MUCH faster than model's 3× acceleration
- Once tipping point passed, dominance (50%) achieved in ~10 years

**Limitations:**
- European data may not generalize to developing markets
- Assumes continued policy support and charging infrastructure
- Does not model supply chain constraints (battery materials)
