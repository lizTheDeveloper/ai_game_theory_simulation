### 3. Lake Erie Eutrophication (PARTIALLY VERIFIED ⚠️)

**Claims:**
- "Dual P/N reduction needed" (Paerl et al. 2024, Limnology and Oceanography)
- "Legacy phosphorus: Sediments release **11,000 metric tons/year** (= total river inputs!)"
- "40% phosphorus reduction goal"

**Verification:**

**Paerl et al. 2024 - CONFIRMED:**
- **Title:** "Dual phosphorus and nitrogen nutrient reduction will be more effective than a phosphorus‐only reduction in mitigating diatom and cyanobacterial blooms in Lake Erie, USA–Canada"
- **Journal:** Limnology and Oceanography, 2024, Volume 69, Issue 12, pages 2913-2928
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1002/lno.12719
- **Authors:** Hans W. Paerl, Justin D. Chaffin, Jack H. Cheshire, et al.

**Phosphorus 11,000 Metric Tons Claim - CONFLATED:**

WebSearch reveals:
> "External TP loading input from rivers and tributaries is approximately **10,000–11,000 metric tons annually**."
>
> "Erie's lake-bottom sediments annually release between **2,000 and 11,500 metric tons of phosphorus**, with the high end of this range equaling the approximate annual inflow of phosphorus to Lake Erie's central basin from rivers and tributaries: 10,000 to 11,000 metric tons."

**Issue:** The document states "Sediments release 11,000 metric tons/year (= total river inputs!)" but research shows:
- **External inputs:** 10,000-11,000 MT/year (rivers/tributaries)
- **Internal loading:** 2,000-11,500 MT/year (sediments)
- The claim conflates these as equivalent when internal loading is actually a RANGE

**Status:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY VERIFIED - Paerl 2024 paper exists and is correctly cited, but phosphorus claim oversimplifies the relationship between external inputs and internal loading ranges.
