## Executive Summary

Ocean acidification represents **Earth's first crossed climate tipping point** (2025), with global coral reefs experiencing irreversible collapse. Research shows pH decline from 8.1 (pre-industrial) → 7.9 (current) drives cascades: coral bleaching → fisheries collapse → food insecurity for 500M-1B people.

**Key Implementation Parameters:**
- **pH Thresholds:** 7.9 (stress, Ω<3.0) | 7.8 (severe, Ω<2.5) | 7.7 (collapse, Ω<2.0)
- **Population at Risk:** 500M-1B globally, 130M in Coral Triangle
- **Economic Value:** $9.9-11T/year (fisheries $6.8B + tourism $19.5B + coastal protection $80B+)
- **2100 Projections:** pH 7.68 (RCP8.5) to 8.06 (RCP1.9)
- **Tipping Point:** Crossed at 1.2-1.4°C warming (2024-2025); recovery requires cooling to ~1°C
- **Coral Loss:** 70-90% by 2050 (1.5°C), >99% by 2100 (2°C)

**Critical Finding:** Even Paris Agreement 1.5°C target results in >99% coral reef loss (very high confidence, IPCC AR6). Recovery requires not just emission cuts but active carbon removal to cool planet.

## 1. pH Thresholds & Tipping Points

### 1.1 Baseline Values

| Period | Surface pH | Aragonite Ω | Source |
|--------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Pre-industrial (1750) | 8.1-8.2 | 4.6 (tropical) | IPCC AR6 WG1, Kleypas & Langdon 2006 |
| Current (2025) | ~7.9 | 3.0-3.5 | Jiang et al. 2023, IPCC AR6 |
| Change | -0.1 to -0.2 | -1.1 to -1.6 | 30% increase in acidity (logarithmic) |

### 1.2 Cascade Thresholds

**MODERATE STRESS: pH < 7.9, Ωar < 3.0**
- **Effects:** Reduced calcification, increased bleaching susceptibility, decreased reproduction
- **Mechanism:** Corals stressed when aragonite saturation < 3 (NOAA Science on a Sphere)
- **Reversibility:** Reversible if stress removed within 1-2 months
- **Uncertainty:** ±0.1 pH units (regional variation)

**SEVERE STRESS: pH < 7.8, Ωar < 2.5**
- **Effects:** Pteropod shell dissolution (37% thickness decline, Bednaršek et al. 2021), synergistic bleaching with warming (Anthony et al. 2008)
- **Timeline:** RCP4.5 by ~2080-2100
- **Reversibility:** Mixed - calcification can recover if pH restored, but decades of stress cause permanent losses

**ECOSYSTEM COLLAPSE: pH < 7.7, Ωar < 2.0**
- **Effects:** Coral growth halts (BGC models), reef shifts to net dissolution (Langdon et al. 2003 at Ω=1.0-2.0)
- **Timeline:** RCP8.5 by ~2100
- **Reversibility:** Effectively irreversible - species extinctions, regime shifts permanent

**ARAGONITE UNDERSATURATION: Ωar < 1.0**
- **Effects:** Active chemical dissolution of shells/skeletons
- **Status:** Polar/deep waters already seasonal; tropical surface not until >2100 (RCP8.5)

## 5. Reversibility & Recovery

### 5.1 Tipping Point Analysis

**Status:** CROSSED at 1.4°C warming (2024-2025)
- Threshold: 1.2°C (range 1.0-1.5°C)
- Meaning: Self-reinforcing decline, annual bleaching prevents recovery
- Source: Nature (2025) DOI: 10.1038/d41586-025-03316-w

### 5.2 Recovery Requirements

**Stabilize at 1.5°C:** >99% loss (INSUFFICIENT)
**Cool to ~1°C:** Reefs could persist at "meaningful scale" if achieved by 2040-2050
**No intervention:** Near-total collapse by 2050-2070, permanent on human timescales

### 5.3 Experimental Evidence

**Lab studies (Albright et al. 2016, Nature):** pH restoration → increased calcification
**Field reality:** Chemical changes "irreversible on centennial to millennial timescales"
**Conclusion:** Local pH improvement CAN help, but ocean-scale reversal requires massive carbon removal

### 5.4 Recovery Timescales (if pH stabilizes/improves)

| Scenario | Coral Recovery | Fisheries Recovery | Irreversible Loss |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|------------------|
| pH stays 7.9 | Decades-centuries | 10-30 years | 10-30% species extinct |
| pH → 8.0 | 30-100 years | 15-40 years | 5-15% species |
| pH → 8.1 + cooling | 50-200 years | 20-50 years | 2-5% species |

**Key Constraint:** Requires pH improvement AND temperature cooling AND local stressor management (pollution, overfishing)

## 6. Integration Considerations

### 6.1 Compound Warming + Acidification (Synergistic)

- **28°C:** Corals cope with higher CO2
- **31°C:** No significant growth even without acidification
- **31°C + pH 7.8:** Severe bleaching within 5 days (Seriatopora hystrix)
- **Mechanism:** Heat disrupts coral ion regulation, makes them vulnerable to external acidification
- **Source:** Anthony et al. (2008) PNAS DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0804478105

### 6.2 Overfishing Interaction

- Herbivore loss → algal overgrowth → smothers coral recruits
- Combined with acidification (already reduces recruitment) → near-zero recovery
- **Management:** Maintain herbivore grazing critical for resilience (Dove et al. 2013 Global Change Biology)

### 6.3 Marine Biodiversity Beyond Corals

- **Pteropods:** Shell dissolution at pH 7.7-7.8, critical prey for fish/whales/seabirds
- **Shellfish:** Reduced growth, larval mortality
- **Crustose coralline algae:** Most sensitive, net dissolution at high CO2
- **Cascades:** Pteropod decline → salmon/whale/seabird food loss

## 7. Ocean Alkalinity Enhancement (Potential Tech)

### 7.1 Concept

Add alkaline substances (quicklime, NaOH, olivine) to seawater to:
1. Raise pH locally
2. Increase atmospheric CO2 uptake
3. Improve aragonite saturation

### 7.2 Evidence

**Lab/mesocosm:** pH restoration → increased calcification (Albright et al. 2016)
**Great Barrier Reef:** Could offset acidification by ~10 years (ScienceDaily 2021)

### 7.3 Feasibility

**Current (2024-2025):** Lab/mesocosm scale only
**2030-2050 potential:** Protected lagoons, coral nurseries (0.1-1 km²), $1-10M/site/year
**Large-scale (2050+):** Unknown - requires cheap alkalinity sources, ecological safety validation
**Constraint:** "Effective only on small scale — protected bays or lagoons" (NCBI, Woods Hole)

## 10. Uncertainties & Contradictions

### High Confidence (IPCC terminology)

✅ Ocean pH declined 0.1-0.2 units since pre-industrial (30% more acidic)
✅ 70-90% coral loss at 1.5°C, >99% at 2°C warming
✅ 500M-1B people depend on reefs for food/income/protection
✅ Warming + acidification = synergistic (more than additive)
✅ Chemical changes irreversible on centennial to millennial timescales

### Moderate Uncertainty

⚠️ **Exact pH thresholds:** pH 7.8-7.9 range, varies by species (±0.1 units)
⚠️ **Recovery timescales:** Decades to centuries (depends on damage extent, pH improvement)
⚠️ **Economic valuations:** $9.9-11T range (±10%)

### High Uncertainty

❓ **Tipping point reversibility:** Depends on extent of damage, local conditions
❓ **Adaptation potential:** Unknown if corals evolve fast enough
❓ **OAE at scale:** Lab success ≠ ocean-basin feasibility

### Contradictions Resolved

1. **Irreversibility:** Ocean-basin chemistry irreversible (centuries) BUT local pH improvement CAN help (scale matters)
2. **Bleaching threshold:** Species-specific (Acropora sensitive, Porites less so)
3. **Population dependence:** 500M (fisheries only) vs 1B (all reef services) - both correct, different scopes

### Recommended Simulation Assumptions

- pH thresholds: 7.9 (stress) | 7.8 (severe) | 7.7 (collapse) ✅ well-supported
- Population: 500M baseline (conservative), scale to 1B if including all services
- Economic: $10T/year (midpoint)
- Irreversibility: Effectively permanent on 2025-2100 timescale
- Warming synergy: 2-3x multiplier when SST > 30°C + pH < 7.9
- OAE: Small-scale only (<2050), not ocean-basin scale
- Tipping point: 1.2°C warming (IPCC), once crossed requires intervention to reverse
