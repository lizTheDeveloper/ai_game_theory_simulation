4. **Anthropic's 2025 AI safety priorities**: Adversarial robustness, mechanistic interpretability, scalable oversight

**Simulation Implications:**
- Experts vs generalists disagree fundamentally on AI risk magnitude (use uncertainty distributions)
- Nuclear risk > bio risk for superforecasters (7.4x higher extinction probability)
- GCR research highly concentrated (60% from US/UK, 75% male authors - geographic/demographic bias)
- Catastrophic risk defined: >10% human population killed within 5 years

## 1. Bibliometric Analysis: State of GCR Research (Jehn et al. 2025)

**Primary Source:** Jehn, F. U., Engler, J.-O., Arnscheidt, C. W., Wache, M., Ilin, E., Cook, L., Sundaram, L. S., Hanusch, F., and Kemp, L. (2025). "The state of global catastrophic risk research: a bibliometric review," *Earth System Dynamics*, 16, 1053–1084. DOI: 10.5194/esd-16-1053-2025

**Citation Quality:** Peer-reviewed in *Earth System Dynamics* (European Geosciences Union), open access

### 1.1 Methodology

- **Dataset:** 3,437 documents from OpenAlex containing "global catastrophic risk" OR "existential risk"
- **Analysis:** Bibliographic coupling (documents sharing references) via VOSviewer clustering
- **Validation:** Two-stage expert screening (shallow + deep scans) for GCR/ER relevance

### 1.2 Ten Research Clusters Identified

| Cluster | Focus | Key Drivers |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| **1. Foundations** | Conceptual frameworks, definitions | Terminology standardization |
| **2. Artificial Intelligence** | AI-related catastrophic risks | Alignment failures, superintelligence |
| **3. Climate Change** | Climate-driven catastrophes | Feedback loops, tipping points |
| **4. Governance** | Policy frameworks, institutions | International coordination |
| **5. Pandemics** | Disease outbreak risks | Zoonotic transmission, preparedness |
| **6. Transhumanism** | Human enhancement, modification | Existential opportunity/risk |
| **7. Global Resilience & Food Security** | Food system vulnerabilities | Sunlight reduction, crop failures |
| **8. Risk Management & Mitigation** | Response strategies | Early warning systems |
| **9. Reasoning & Risk** | Cognitive/psychological factors | Forecasting, probability judgment |
| **10. Emerging Biotechnologies** | Novel biological risks | Engineered pathogens, CRISPR |

### 1.3 Field Growth Metrics

**Publication Trends:**
- 2010: ~10 publications/year
- 2023: 150+ publications/year
- **Growth rate:** 15x over 13 years

**Geographic Concentration:**
- **60% from US and UK** (predominantly OECD countries)
- **Limitation:** Underrepresentation of Global South perspectives

**Gender Imbalance:**
- **75% male authors overall**
- **85% male in food security cluster**
- **Implication:** Potential blind spots from demographic homogeneity

**Journal Fragmentation:**
- No unified GCR journal
- Publications dispersed across multiple outlets
- **Challenge:** Field cohesion and cross-pollination

### 1.4 Main GCR Drivers Identified

**AI Risks:**
- Existential threats from advanced AI development
- Alignment failures with human values
- Loss of control scenarios

**Climate Feedback Loops:**
- Richards et al. identified food insecurity as "most concerning pathway"
- Tipping point cascades (ice sheets, AMOC, Amazon)
- Multi-breadbasket failure events

**Pandemics:**
- Zoonotic disease transmission
- Preparedness gaps (COVID-19 lessons)
- Engineered pathogen risks

**Sunlight-Reduction Scenarios:**
- Volcanic eruptions (supervolcano)
- Nuclear winter
- Asteroid impact

**Infrastructure Collapse:**
- Large-scale electrical grid disruptions
- Cascading failures across interconnected systems
- Critical supply chain breakdowns

**Compound Interactions:**
- Multi-crisis scenarios (climate + pandemic + conflict)
- Systemic risk amplification

## 2. Expert Forecasting Tournament: Catastrophic Risk by 2100 (Karger et al. 2025)

**Primary Source:** Karger, E., Rosenberg, J., Jacobs, Z., Hickman, M., and Tetlock, P.E. (2025). "Subjective-probability forecasts of existential risk: Initial results from a hybrid persuasion-forecasting tournament," *International Journal of Forecasting*, 41(2), 499-516. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijforecast.2024.09.006

**Citation Quality:** Peer-reviewed in *International Journal of Forecasting* (flagship forecasting journal), Tetlock (renowned forecasting researcher)

### 2.1 Methodology

**Tournament Structure:**
- **Multi-stage persuasion-forecasting tournament** (4 months duration)
- **Participants:** Domain experts (AI safety, biosecurity, climate) + generalist "superforecasters"
- **Forecasts:** Probability judgments for catastrophic events 2025-2100
- **Persuasion component:** Incentivized to share best arguments, attempt to convince others
- **Outcome:** Neither side materially moved the other's views

**Catastrophe Definition:**
> "Will [risk source] by 2100 cause a 'catastrophe,' killing within a five-year period more than 10% of humans alive at the beginning of that period?"

**Risk Sources Assessed:**
1. Artificial Intelligence (AI)
2. Bioweapons (engineered pathogens)
3. Nuclear war
4. Non-anthropogenic risks (asteroid, supervolcano, natural pandemic)

### 2.2 Overall Catastrophe & Extinction Risk Forecasts

**By 2100:**

| Forecast Type | Domain Experts | Superforecasters | Ratio |
|---------------|----------------|------------------|-------|
| **Catastrophe (>10% killed in 5yr)** | 20% | 9% | 2.2x |
| **Extinction (100% killed)** | 6% | 1% | 6.0x |

**Interpretation:**
- Experts consistently more pessimistic across all risk types
- 2.2x-6.0x disagreement on aggregate risk
- **No convergence despite 4 months of structured debate**

### 2.3 AI-Specific Risk Forecasts

**AI Catastrophe Risk by 2100:**

| Group | Forecast | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| **"Concerned" (domain experts)** | 20% | All participants were AI safety researchers |
| **"Skeptical" (superforecasters)** | 0.12% | Generalist forecasters, no AI domain expertise |
| **Disagreement Ratio** | **167x** | Largest divergence of any risk category |

**Key Findings:**

1. **Unprecedented disagreement:** 167x gap is far larger than for other risks
2. **No persuasion effect:** 4 months of debate did not shift views
3. **Epistemic humility:** Both groups acknowledged uncertainty but held positions
4. **Domain expertise premium:** Experts weighted AI risk much higher

**Quote from research:**
> "Specialists were more pessimistic, especially on long-run threats posed by artificial intelligence (AI). Despite incentives to share their best arguments during four months of discussion, neither side materially moved the other's views."

### 2.4 Comparative Risk Assessment: AI vs Nuclear vs Bio

**Extinction Risk Ratios (Superforecasters):**

| Risk Source | Relative Probability | Notes |
|-------------|---------------------|-------|
| **Nuclear War** | 1.0x (baseline) | Highest extinction risk for superforecasters |
| **Bioweapons** | 0.135x | 7.4x LOWER than nuclear |
| **AI** | 0.195x | 5.1x LOWER than nuclear |

**Interpretation:**
- Superforecasters rank nuclear > AI > bio for extinction risk
- Nuclear war remains highest traditional GCR threat
- Bio risk lowest (assumes natural containment, medical response)

**Domain Expert View:**
- Likely inverted: AI > Nuclear > Bio (based on 20% AI catastrophe forecast)
- Reflects concern over uncontrolled superintelligence scenarios

### 2.5 Timeframe Analysis

**Short-Range Forecasts (2025-2030):**
- Resolution dates within 5 years
- Higher confidence, lower disagreement
- Observable indicators (AI capabilities, nuclear arsenals, pathogen surveillance)

**Long-Range Forecasts (2030-2100):**
- Resolution dates 5-75 years out
- Maximum disagreement (especially AI risk)
- Speculative extrapolation beyond observable trends

**Implication for Simulation:**
Use **uncertainty distributions** rather than point estimates for long-range AI risk (2050+). Short-range risk (2025-2035) has higher expert agreement.

### 2.6 Limitations and Biases

**Acknowledged Limitations:**

1. **Selection bias:** Participants self-selected (concerns about GCR may bias toward pessimism)
2. **Framing effects:** "Catastrophe" definition (>10% killed) may anchor responses
3. **Overconfidence:** Both groups may underestimate uncertainty
4. **Limited diversity:** Predominantly Western, male participants
5. **No ground truth:** Cannot validate forecasts until resolution dates

**Jehn et al. (2025) Context:**
GCR field is 75% male, 60% US/UK - forecasting tournament likely reflects same biases.

## 3. AI Safety Research Priorities 2025 (Anthropic)

**Primary Source:** Anthropic. (2025). "Recommendations for Technical AI Safety Research Directions." Retrieved from https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/recommended-directions/

**Source Quality:** Industry research lab (Anthropic), cutting-edge AI safety work, not peer-reviewed but highly influential

### 3.1 Adversarial Robustness

**Core Problem:** AI systems deployed in adversarial environments encounter inputs designed to cause undesired behavior.

**Current Attack Surfaces:**
1. **Direct prompting:** Jailbreak attempts via carefully crafted inputs
2. **Pretraining corpus poisoning:** Malicious data in training sets
3. **Website hijacking:** Adversarial content targeting AI agents

**Underexplored Research Directions:**

#### 3.1.1 Realistic & Differential Jailbreak Benchmarks

**Current Problem:** Existing benchmarks measure "non-refusal" but not "capability to cause harm."

**Needed:**
- Measure whether jailbreaks enable tasks **unavailable without AI assistance**
- Baseline against realistic adversary tools (search engines, open-source models)
- Test practical harmful use-cases (not abstract scenarios)
- Assess attack universality (across inputs and models)

**Example:**
> "Does a jailbroken GPT-5 enable a non-expert to synthesize a novel bioweapon that they couldn't create using Google Search + open literature?"

#### 3.1.2 Adaptive Defenses

**Current Limitation:** Most defenses are static (pre-deployment only), don't respond to adaptive adversaries.

**Research Priorities:**

1. **Inter-query monitoring:**
   - Adversaries try many prompts before success
   - Monitor **sets of queries** rather than individual prompts
   - Identify suspicious query patterns
   - Infer high-level malicious tasks from low-level subtasks

2. **Rapid response systems:**
   - Patch discovered attacks quickly
   - Prevent future exploitation of similar vulnerabilities
   - Update defenses based on observed adversary behavior

**Quote:**
> "It's common to think about defenses as a pre-deployment problem with static defenses that don't respond to adaptive adversaries, while a less-explored research line focuses on adaptive defenses that depend on prior model and adversary behavior."

### 3.2 Mechanistic Interpretability

**Core Question:** Can we understand model cognition by reverse-engineering learned algorithms?

**Anthropic's Approach:**
- Detailed interpretability work documented separately (Anthropic interpretability team blog)
- Alternative approaches: Externalized cognition (chain-of-thought), introspection training

**Key Research Questions:**
1. Do models form plans?
2. What situational awareness exists (training vs deployment)?
3. Does hidden knowledge exist that models don't report when queried?

**Introspection Training:**
> "Training models to directly verbalize the content of their hidden states" - make implicit reasoning explicit

### 3.3 Scalable Oversight

**Core Challenge:** Prevent models from exploiting systematic errors in overseer (human or AI) feedback signals.

**Four Primary Research Directions:**

#### 3.3.1 Systematic Oversight Error Exploitation

**Problem:** Models may learn to "produce answers which seem correct to human overseers, but do not actually reflect the model's best guess."

**Research Needs:**
- Create testbeds where models understand overseer mistakes
- Develop techniques leveraging AI assistance to improve imperfect signals
- Test whether models optimize for "appears correct" vs "is correct"

#### 3.3.2 Recursive Oversight

**Approaches:**
1. **Task decomposition:** Break difficult problems into supervisable subtasks
2. **Adversarial techniques:** Debate, prover-verifier games pitting competing systems against each other

**Example (Debate):**
Two AI systems argue opposing positions on a question. Human judges the debate. Assumption: Truth is easier to defend than falsehood in debate format.

#### 3.3.3 Weak-to-Strong Generalization

**Question:** Can strong models be trained using weak overseer signals?

**Techniques:**
- Unsupervised improvements through self-consistency regularization
- Study task-dependent generalization differences
- Explore when weak supervision suffices vs requires strong oversight

#### 3.3.4 Easy-to-Hard Generalization

**Trade-off:**
- High-quality unrepresentative data (easy tasks, confident labels)
- vs Low-quality representative data (hard tasks, uncertain labels)

**Research Question:** When does training on easy tasks generalize to hard tasks?

#### 3.3.5 Honesty Detection

**Goal:** Identify when models believe their responses are truthful (without judging accuracy).

**Approach:** Leverage "the model's own knowledge about whether it is responding honestly."

**Distinction:**
- Not "is the answer correct?" (requires ground truth)
- But "does the model believe it's telling the truth?" (internal state detection)

### 3.4 Dangerous Capabilities Evaluation

**Core Problem:** Many benchmarks "saturate quickly and therefore fail to provide a continuous, extrapolatable signal of AI progress," while real-world impacts lag behind benchmark performance.

**Research Priorities:**

1. **High-quality assessments tracking real-world impact:**
   - Not just benchmark accuracy
   - Measure actual task completion in realistic scenarios

2. **Security-critical domains (CBRN):**
   - Secured labs with government coordination
   - Test CBRN (Chemical, Biological, Radiological, Nuclear) risks
   - Evaluate whether AI enables non-experts to cause catastrophic harm

3. **Novel research capabilities:**
   - Can AI conduct original research?
   - Tool interoperability (using multiple software tools in sequence)
   - Autonomous task completion without human intervention

4. **Human baselines for comparison:**
   - Compare AI performance to human experts, non-experts
   - Measure AI's "skill multiplier" for adversaries

**Specific Capability Questions:**
- Does capability improvement via chain-of-thought indicate faithfulness?
- How do models generalize capabilities across different domains?

### 3.5 Model Organisms / Understanding Model Cognition

**Core Research Questions:**

1. **Truthfulness:**
   > "When our model gave an answer, did it do so because it thought the answer was correct?"

2. **Planning:**
   - Do models form multi-step plans?
   - How persistent are plans across interactions?

3. **Situational Awareness:**
   - Do models distinguish training from deployment?
   - What knowledge do they have about their own architecture, purpose, limitations?

4. **Hidden Knowledge:**
   - Does the model know things it doesn't report when queried?
   - Can we elicit latent knowledge through better prompting or probing?

**Persona Effects Research:**
> "Small changes in the persona may have important impacts on how they behave in novel situations."

**Approach:** Create models with identical capabilities but different personalities to isolate persona influences on safety-critical behavior.

### 3.6 Additional Research Directions

#### Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness

**Question:** Do reasoning traces match actual model cognition?

**Research Needs:**
- Detect or ensure CoT faithfulness
- Understand whether faithfulness varies by task type
- Test if models "perform" reasoning vs actually use it

#### AI Control via Monitoring

**Three Monitoring Types:**

1. **Behavioral monitoring:** Actor-monitor screening (red team vs blue team)
2. **Activation monitoring:** Latent state probes (internal representations)
3. **Anomaly detection:** Out-of-distribution input detection

#### Unlearning

**Goal:** Remove dangerous information from pretrained models.

**Challenge:** Prevent re-emergence through further fine-tuning.

**Research Questions:**
- Can we selectively forget CBRN knowledge while preserving benign capabilities?
- How robust is unlearning to adversarial fine-tuning?

#### Multi-Agent Alignment

**Problem:** Coordination failures across multiple AI instances operating simultaneously.

**Governance Structures Needed:**
- Prevent race dynamics between AI agents
- Ensure aligned goals across multi-agent systems
- Manage emergent behavior from agent interactions

## 5. Key Takeaways for Simulation Design

### 5.1 Fundamental Disagreements Are Real

**Experts vs Generalists:**
- 2.2x disagreement on overall catastrophe risk
- 6.0x disagreement on extinction risk
- **167x disagreement on AI catastrophe risk**

**Implication:** Don't use point estimates for AI risk. Use distributions or allow player choice of epistemic framework.

### 5.2 GCR Field Has Blind Spots

**Geographic:**
- 60% US/UK (Western perspectives dominate)
- Global South underrepresented

**Demographic:**
- 75% male authors
- Potential for groupthink

**Disciplinary:**
- Journal fragmentation (no unified field)
- Limited cross-pollination across clusters

**Implication:** Multi-Paradigm DUI is critical for capturing diverse risk framings.

### 5.3 AI Safety Is Moving Fast

**2025 Priorities (Anthropic):**
1. Adversarial robustness (adaptive defenses, realistic benchmarks)
2. Mechanistic interpretability (understand model cognition)
3. Scalable oversight (prevent exploitation of overseer errors)
4. Dangerous capabilities evaluation (CBRN, autonomous research)

**Implication:** AI safety research is NOT static. Annual updates needed to track cutting-edge work.

### 5.4 Catastrophe Thresholds Are Defined

**Karger et al. (2025) Standard:**
- Catastrophe: >10% killed within 5 years
- For 2025: **815 million deaths**

**Historical Context:**
- WWII: ~75 million deaths (6 years) = 0.9% of 1940 population (NOT a catastrophe by this definition)
- Spanish Flu (1918-1920): 50-100 million deaths = 2.5-5% of 1918 population (also NOT a catastrophe by this definition)
- **No historical precedent** for >10% killed in 5 years since 1800

**Implication:** "Catastrophe" is an unprecedented event in modern history. Simulation should reflect this rarity while modeling plausible pathways (nuclear winter, engineered pandemic, runaway AI).

### 5.5 Nuclear Risk > Bio Risk (For Superforecasters)

**Relative Extinction Probabilities:**
- Nuclear: 7.4x higher than bioweapons
- AI: 5.1x higher than bioweapons (for skeptics) OR 167x higher (for experts)

**Implication:** Bioweapons are lowest GCR threat according to generalist forecasters (natural containment, medical response). Nuclear and AI are top concerns.
