## DETAILED CLAIM-BY-CLAIM VERIFICATION

### SOURCE 1: Nature Scientific Reports (2025) - "The effectiveness of explainable AI on human factors in trust models"

**Citation Claim (Lines 22-27):**
> While explainability has received significant research attention, empirical evidence shows **inconsistent and context-dependent effects** on trust
> High-stakes domains: Explanations can **decrease trust** when they reveal concerning decision processes

**Verification Status:** ⚠️ **PARTIALLY VERIFIED - CRITICAL MISREPRESENTATION**

**Direct Source Access:**
- **DOI:** s41598-025-04189-9 ✅ VERIFIED
- **Authors:** Justin C. Cheung, Shirley S. Ho (Nanyang Technological University, Singapore) ✅ VERIFIED
- **Publication Date:** July 2, 2025 (Received Nov 28, 2024; Accepted May 26, 2025) ✅ VERIFIED
- **Sample:** N=1,002 Singapore residents ✅ VERIFIED
- **Context:** Autonomous passenger drones, public opinion survey ✅ VERIFIED

**Actual Findings from Source:**
The paper states:
> "Through a public opinion survey in Singapore (N = 1,002), structural equation modelling analyses revealed that **perceived explainability significantly shaped all trust in AI engineers' dimensions** (i.e., ability, benevolence, and integrity)."

**CRITICAL DISTINCTION:**
The paper studies **trust in AI ENGINEERS**, NOT **trust in AI systems**. The research question is:
> "whether explainable AI communication could improve trust in AI, particularly exploring **trust in AI engineers** as an under-explored dimension of trust"

**Key Quote:**
> "Ho and Cheung argued that there exists a need to conceptually differentiate between **trust in AI system and trust in AI engineers**. Trust in AI system primarily relates to systemic attributes, whereas trust in AI engineers concerns human attributes."

**Finding:** The paper found **POSITIVE effects of perceived explainability on trust in AI engineers** (ability, benevolence, integrity dimensions). The claim that "explanations can decrease trust when they reveal concerning decision processes" is **NOT SUPPORTED** by this source.

**Verdict:** 🚨 **CRITICAL MISREPRESENTATION** - The source found positive effects of explainability on trust in engineers, NOT negative effects on system trust in high-stakes domains.
