## Executive Summary

The god mode test revealed that climate tech effectiveness is **energy-constrained**: deploying DAC, cleanup technologies, and industrial decarbonization simultaneously requires MORE clean energy than currently available. Three breakthrough categories could resolve this bottleneck:

1. **Early Fusion Deployment (Accelerated Timeline):** ITER now delayed to 2034 (deuterium plasma), 2039 (D-T operation) - a DECADE behind schedule. However, aggressive public investment (Manhattan Project analog) could accelerate DEMO → commercial pathway from 2050+ to 2035-2040. This would provide massive baseload clean energy WHEN NEEDED (2030s-2040s), not after the climate window closes (2050+). Key finding: Fusion is not "perpetually 30 years away" - it's engineering + financing, not physics.

2. **Perovskite Tandem Solar (40-50% Efficiency):** Current silicon solar: 20-25% efficiency. Perovskite-silicon tandems: 34.85% world record (Longi, April 2025). Commercial deployment starting NOW (Oxford PV shipped 24.5% panels to US, Sept 2024). Industry projects 29%+ stable efficiency in production by early 2025. **Effect: Double energy per panel** → halve land/material requirements → faster deployment. Market forecast: 1 GW production end 2024, 6 GW in 2025, 5-10% global solar market by 2030.

3. **Carbon-Negative Building Materials:** Construction sector is 8-11% of global CO₂ emissions. Carbon-negative alternatives (biochar concrete, engineered wood, carbon-mineralized cement) can turn construction into a CARBON SINK. Biochar concrete: Sequester 59 kg CO₂/tonne with 30% biochar + 9% metakaolin. Engineered wood: LP Building Solutions reports 91% of 2024 sales are carbon-negative products. US market: $3.9B (2024) → $12B (2034) at 11-12% CAGR.

**KEY FINDING:** These three breakthroughs address the energy bottleneck from different angles: (1) fusion provides baseload abundance, (2) perovskite solar accelerates renewable deployment, (3) carbon-negative materials reduce demand while sequestering CO₂. Together, they shift energy from constraint to enabler.

## Technology Catalog

### 1. Early Fusion Deployment (Accelerated ITER → DEMO → Commercial)

**Type:** Magnetic Confinement Fusion Energy (Tokamak)

**The Energy Bottleneck:**
- **DAC energy requirement:** 10 GtCO₂/year removal = 10,000-22,000 TWh/year
- **Current global renewable electricity:** ~20,000 TWh/year (2024)
- **Implication:** Scaling DAC to climate-relevant levels requires DOUBLING global renewable capacity
- **Alternative:** Fusion provides baseload clean energy without land/material constraints of renewables

**Current State (2024-2025 ITER Delays):**

**Major Setback (October 2024):**
- **ITER timeline extended:** Deuterium-only plasma now 2034 (was 2025); D-T operation 2039 (was 2035)
- **Cost overrun:** Additional €5B
- **Cause:** "Massive decade-long delay" (Physics World) - engineering/coordination challenges, not physics
- **Implication:** Fusion arriving AFTER climate window closes (need solutions by 2030-2040, not 2050+)

**BUT: Engineering Problem, Not Physics Problem**

**Senate Testimony (2024):**
- After ITER delay, US senators questioned fusion experts on commercial reactor timelines
- **Consensus:** Physics proven (Q>1 achieved); challenge is engineering + financing
- **Private sector:** 30+ fusion startups pursuing alternative designs (not just ITER tokamak)
- **Funding gap:** ITER is international mega-project (slow, consensus-driven); needs Manhattan Project-scale urgency

**Accelerated Pathway (With 10× Funding):**

**Conventional Timeline:**
- ITER (2025-2039): Demonstrate net energy gain at scale
- DEMO (2040-2050): Demonstrate electricity generation
- Commercial (2050-2060): First commercial fusion plants
- **Total: 35+ years from 2025**

**Accelerated Timeline (Manhattan Project Analog):**
- **ITER completion:** 2027-2030 (parallel construction, not sequential; more resources)
- **DEMO programs:** 2028-2035 (multiple designs in parallel: K-DEMO (Korea), CFETR (China), EU DEMO, US private sector)
- **Early commercial:** 2035-2040 (first-generation plants, learning by doing)
- **Total: 10-15 years from 2025** (3× faster)

**Mechanism for Acceleration:**
- **Funding:** $100-200B over 10 years (currently ~$20B/year globally) = 5-10× increase
- **Parallel development:** Multiple DEMO designs simultaneously (not wait for ITER, then build DEMO)
- **Private sector:** Leverage 30+ startups (Commonwealth Fusion, TAE Technologies, Helion, etc.)
- **Regulatory streamlining:** Fast-track fusion plant licensing (fusion is inherently safer than fission)
- **Manhattan Project analog:** WW2 bomb: $2B (1945 dollars) = ~$30B today; 3 years from start to Trinity test

**Energy Output at Scale:**
- **Fusion plant capacity:** 500-1,000 MW electric (similar to large fission reactor)
- **Capacity factor:** 80-90% (baseload, not intermittent like solar/wind)
- **Number of plants needed:** 10,000 plants × 1,000 MW × 0.85 CF = 74,400 TWh/year (3.7× current global electricity)
- **Timeline to this scale:** 2040-2060 (even with acceleration, buildout takes decades)

**Timeline:**
- **2025-2030:** Aggressive R&D investment; multiple DEMO pathways
- **2030-2035:** First DEMO plants operational; prove commercial viability
- **2035-2040:** Early commercial plants (10-100 reactors); learning curve begins
- **2040-2050:** Scale-up phase (100-1,000 reactors); costs declining
- **2050-2060:** Mature industry (1,000-10,000 reactors); fusion = major energy source

**Effectiveness:**
- **Energy abundance:** Fusion removes energy constraint on climate tech (DAC, electrification, desalination)
- **Baseload:** Unlike solar/wind, fusion provides 24/7 power (no storage needed)
- **Land footprint:** Minimal vs. solar/wind (fusion plant is compact)
- **Fuel abundance:** Deuterium from seawater (virtually unlimited); lithium for tritium breeding

**Dependencies:**
- **Political will:** $100-200B investment over 10 years (government + private)
- **International cooperation:** ITER partners (EU, US, China, Russia, Japan, Korea, India) must coordinate
- **Engineering talent:** 10× scale-up requires workforce expansion
- **Regulatory frameworks:** Fusion-specific licensing (not fission regulations)
- **Public acceptance:** Fusion ≠ fission (no meltdown risk, minimal waste), but education needed

**Challenges:**
- **ITER delays show:** Engineering is HARD (plasma control, materials science, superconducting magnets)
- **Neutron damage:** Reactor walls degrade over time (materials science challenge)
- **Tritium breeding:** Must breed own tritium from lithium (not yet demonstrated at scale)
- **Cost uncertainty:** First-generation plants may be expensive ($10-20B per reactor); learning curve critical

**Research Confidence:** B+ (physics proven; ITER delays confirm engineering challenges; acceleration possible with funding; timelines based on Manhattan Project analog; private sector progress encouraging)

### 2. Advanced Solar: Perovskite-Silicon Tandem Cells

**Type:** High-Efficiency Photovoltaic (40-50% efficiency potential)

**The Efficiency Gap:**
- **Current silicon solar:** 20-25% efficiency (theoretical limit ~29%)
- **Current deployment bottleneck:** Land area, material availability, installation labor
- **Implication:** Doubling efficiency → halve land/materials → faster deployment

**Perovskite-Silicon Tandem Technology:**

**World Record Efficiency (April 2025):**
- **Longi (China):** 34.85% efficiency (two-terminal perovskite-silicon tandem)
- **Previous records:**
  - LONGi (June 2024): 34.6%
  - JinkoSolar: 33.84%
  - Taiwan Academia Sinica: 31.5%
  - Japan EneCoat Technologies: 30.4%
- **Trend:** Rapid improvement (30% → 34.85% in ~2 years)

**Commercial Deployment (2024-2025):**

**Oxford PV (UK):**
- **First commercial shipment:** September 2024 to US utility-scale installation
- **Efficiency:** 24.5% (tandem 72-cell panels)
- **Production:** Plans to scale to gigawatt capacity
- **Significance:** Proof that tandem cells work at commercial scale (not just lab records)

**Market Projections:**
- **S&P Global:** 1 GW production end 2024, 6 GW in 2025 (6× growth)
- **Industry analysts:** Stable 29%+ efficiency in production by early 2025
- **Market penetration:** 5-10% of global solar by 2030, 25% by 2035
- **Timeline:** "Meaningful commercial deployment 2025-2027; mass adoption 2028-2030"

**Mechanism:**
- **Tandem architecture:** Perovskite layer (top) captures high-energy photons; silicon layer (bottom) captures low-energy photons
- **Energy harvesting:** Two bandgaps → capture broader spectrum than silicon alone
- **Efficiency gain:** 40-60% improvement over silicon-only (20-25% → 30-35% commercial, 40%+ theoretical)

**Timeline:**
- **2024-2025:** Early commercial deployment (GW scale production)
- **2025-2030:** Rapid scaling (10-50 GW production; costs declining)
- **2030-2035:** Mainstream adoption (perovskite-silicon becomes dominant solar tech)
- **2035-2040:** Maturity (50-90% of new solar installations use tandem cells)
- **Total: 5-15 years** to market dominance

**Cost Dynamics:**
- **Current (2024):** Perovskite-silicon more expensive than silicon-only (new manufacturing processes)
- **Learning curve:** Costs decline 15-25% per doubling of capacity (standard for solar)
- **Crossover point:** 2027-2030 (tandem cells cost-competitive with silicon)
- **Long-term:** Tandem cells cheaper per watt (higher efficiency offsets manufacturing complexity)

**Deployment Acceleration:**
- **Land requirements:** 40% efficiency vs. 20% → half the land area for same output
- **Material efficiency:** Fewer panels → less silicon, aluminum, glass, copper
- **Installation labor:** Half the panels → half the installation time
- **Net effect: 2-3× faster solar deployment** (same land/material/labor → double output)

**Challenges:**
- **Stability:** Perovskite degrades with moisture, UV, heat (Oxford PV addressed with encapsulation)
- **Lead content:** Environmental concern (lead-free alternatives in development, but lower efficiency)
- **Manufacturing scaling:** New processes vs. established silicon supply chain
- **Certification:** Long-term reliability testing for 25-30 year warranties

**Co-Benefits:**
- **Agrivoltaics:** Higher efficiency → more space between panels → crops grow underneath (dual land use)
- **Building-integrated PV:** Higher efficiency enables smaller installations (rooftops, facades)
- **Space power:** Ultra-high efficiency critical for satellites, lunar/Mars bases (spin-off applications)

**Research Confidence:** A- (commercial products shipping; world record 34.85%; market forecasts from S&P; manufacturing scale-up confirmed; challenges documented)

### 3. Carbon-Negative Building Materials

**Type:** Construction Materials That Sequester More CO₂ Than Emitted in Production

**The Construction Emissions Problem:**
- **Global construction emissions:** 8-11% of global CO₂ (cement = 8%; buildings = additional 3%)
- **Cement production:** 1.5 kg CO₂ per kg cement (thermal decomposition of limestone)
- **Annual cement:** ~4.4 billion tonnes → 6.6 Gt CO₂/year
- **Implication:** Can't reach net-zero without transforming construction sector

**Three Carbon-Negative Pathways:**

#### A. Biochar Concrete

**Mechanism:**
- **Biochar:** Pyrolyzed biomass (wood, agricultural waste) = stable carbon (millennium timescales)
- **Replacement:** Substitute 20-30% of cement with biochar (reduces cement emissions)
- **Sequestration:** Biochar embeds carbon in concrete (permanent storage)
- **Net carbon:** Negative (biochar sequesters more CO₂ than concrete production emits)

**Performance (Peer-Reviewed Research 2024):**
- **30% biochar + 9% metakaolin:** Sequester 59 kg CO₂ per tonne of concrete
- **20% biochar:** Net-zero emissions (carbon storage offsets production)
- **Trade-offs:** Biochar reduces compressive strength; metakaolin compensates (pozzolanic reaction)

**Commercial Status (2024):**
- **Research stage → early commercial:** Biochar concrete specifications in development
- **Market size:** US carbon-sequestering materials market $3.9B (2024) → $12B (2034) at 11-12% CAGR
- **Adoption:** Green building standards (LEED, BREEAM) incentivizing carbon-negative materials

**Timeline:**
- **2024-2030:** Building code approvals; pilot construction projects
- **2030-2040:** Mainstream adoption (10-30% of new construction)
- **2040-2050:** Maturity (50-80% of new construction uses carbon-negative materials)
- **Total: 10-20 years** to significant market penetration

#### B. Engineered Wood (Mass Timber)

**Mechanism:**
- **Wood sequesters CO₂:** Trees absorb CO₂ during growth; carbon stored in wood
- **Substitution:** Replace steel/concrete in buildings with cross-laminated timber (CLT), glulam
- **Carbon accounting:** Wood building stores ~50-100 kg CO₂/m³; concrete building emits ~150-200 kg CO₂/m³
- **Net carbon:** 200-300 kg CO₂/m³ avoided + sequestered = carbon-negative

**Commercial Status (2024-2025):**
- **LP Building Solutions:** 91% of 2024 sales are carbon-negative products
- **21 plants globally:** Manufacturing CLT, engineered wood products
- **Market growth:** Timber/engineered wood projected USD 1.3B (2024) → growth at 12.3% CAGR through 2034

**Performance:**
- **Structural capacity:** CLT can replace concrete in buildings up to ~18 stories (proven in Europe)
- **Fire resistance:** Treated wood meets fire codes (char layer protects inner wood)
- **Seismic performance:** Wood is flexible (excellent earthquake resistance)

**Barriers:**
- **Building codes:** Many jurisdictions restrict wood in tall buildings (legacy fire concerns)
- **Supply chain:** Sustainable forestry scaling (can't clearcut old-growth; need plantation management)
- **Architect familiarity:** Design standards, construction practices differ from steel/concrete

**Timeline:**
- **2024-2030:** Code revisions allowing taller wood buildings; supply chain scaling
- **2030-2040:** Mass timber becomes common for mid-rise buildings (5-15 stories)
- **2040-2050:** Tall wood buildings (15-30 stories) in major cities
- **Total: 10-20 years** to mainstream adoption

#### C. Carbon-Mineralized Cement

**Mechanism:**
- **CO₂ injection:** Bubble CO₂ into concrete mix during curing (mineralization)
- **Chemical reaction:** CO₂ reacts with calcium ions → calcium carbonate (limestone) → permanent sequestration
- **Strength enhancement:** Mineralization improves concrete strength (bonus benefit)
- **Net carbon:** 20-30% cement replacement with carbon pellets → net-zero emissions at scale

**Performance (2024 Research):**
- **20% carbon pellets:** Net-zero emissions (storage offsets production)
- **Energy requirement:** Low (CO₂ injection at ambient pressure; no high-temperature curing)

**Commercial Status (2024):**
- **Pilot projects:** CarbonCure (Canada), Solidia (US), CarbiCrete (Canada)
- **Adoption:** 500+ concrete plants using CO₂ injection (growing rapidly)
- **Market size:** Part of $3.9B US carbon-sequestering materials market

**Timeline:**
- **2024-2027:** Regulatory approvals; demonstration projects
- **2027-2035:** Mainstream adoption (30-60% of concrete production)
- **2035-2045:** Industry standard (80-90% of concrete is carbon-mineralized)
- **Total: 10-20 years** to market dominance

**Aggregate Carbon Impact:**
- **Global concrete:** 4.4 Gt/year × 30% biochar/mineralization = 1.3 Gt CO₂ sequestered/year
- **Engineered wood:** Substitute 20% of new construction → 0.5 Gt CO₂ avoided/year
- **Total potential: 1.8 Gt CO₂/year** (equivalent to 18% of current emissions)

**Dependencies:**
- **Biomass availability:** Biochar requires sustainable forestry/agricultural waste (not competition with food/land)
- **Building codes:** Update fire, structural, durability standards for new materials
- **Supply chains:** Scale production from pilot → industrial volumes
- **Cost parity:** Carbon-negative materials must be cost-competitive (or carbon pricing makes them cheaper)

**Research Confidence:** B+ (biochar performance peer-reviewed; engineered wood commercial (LP 91% of sales); carbon-mineralization operational (500+ plants); market growth confirmed; timelines based on historical material adoption rates)

```typescript
    baseRenewable *= (1 + 1.0 * perovskiteMaturity);  // Up to 2× at full maturity
  }

  // Fusion: Add baseload capacity
  if (state.technologies.earlyFusion.deployed) {
    const fusionPlants = calculateFusionPlants(state.yearsSinceDeployment);
    const fusionEnergy = fusionPlants * 1000 * 0.90 * 8760 / 1000;  // MW → TWh/year
    baseRenewable += fusionEnergy;
  }

  // Carbon-negative materials: Reduce construction sector demand
  if (state.technologies.carbonNegativeMaterials.deployed) {
    const demandReduction = calculateConstructionDemandReduction(state);
    // Effectively increases surplus (same generation, lower demand)
    baseRenewable += demandReduction;
  }

  return baseRenewable;
}
```

### 3. DAC Effectiveness Gated by Energy Availability

```typescript
// DAC effectiveness limited by available energy surplus
function calculateDACEffectiveness(state: GameState): number {
  const cleanEnergy = calculateCleanEnergy(state);
  const essentialDemand = calculateEssentialDemand(state);
  const surplus = cleanEnergy - essentialDemand;

  // DAC energy requirement: 1,000-2,200 kWh per ton CO₂
  const dacEnergyRequired = state.dac.targetCapacity * 1500;  // kWh per ton (median)

  // Effectiveness capped by energy availability
  const energyConstrainedEffectiveness = Math.min(1.0, surplus / dacEnergyRequired);

  return state.dac.baseEffectiveness * energyConstrainedEffectiveness;
}
```
