### 2. Claim Verification: ❌ **CLAIM NOT SUPPORTED**

**What the 1995 Paper Actually Covers:**

The Mayer et al. (1995) paper is a **foundational model of trust formation**, NOT trust restoration or repair. It addresses:

1. **Definition of Trust:** "The willingness of a party to be vulnerable to the actions of another party based on the expectation that the other will perform a particular action important to the trustor, irrespective of the ability to monitor or control that other party"

2. **Three Factors of Trustworthiness:**
   - **Ability:** Skills, competencies, and characteristics enabling influence within a domain
   - **Benevolence:** Positive personal orientation of the trustee toward the trustor
   - **Integrity:** Adherence to principles the trustor finds acceptable

3. **Propensity to Trust:** Individual difference in general willingness to trust others

4. **Risk-Taking Relationship:** Trust leads to risk-taking behaviors in relationships

5. **Propositions:** Several research propositions about how trustworthiness factors interact with risk

**What It Does NOT Cover:**
- ❌ Trust violations
- ❌ Trust betrayal
- ❌ Trust repair mechanisms
- ❌ Trust restoration after damage
- ❌ Recovery from trust breaches

The authors revisited their 1995 model and explicitly stated:

**Interpretation:** In 2007, they acknowledged that trust violation and repair were **NEW research areas** that had emerged AFTER their 1995 paper. This confirms the 1995 paper did not address these topics.

**Timeline of trust repair literature:**

**Mid-1990s onwards:** Trust repair became an important theoretical concern for researchers.

**Key foundational papers (AFTER Mayer 1995):**
- Robinson (1996) - Trust violations

**Conclusion:** Trust repair research emerged in the late 1990s-2000s, building on foundational models like Mayer et al. (1995), but the 1995 paper itself does not address repair/restoration.

## What Mayer et al. (1995) IS Useful For

The paper IS highly relevant to trust dynamics in the simulation, but for **trust FORMATION**, not restoration:

1. **Three-factor trustworthiness model** (ability, benevolence, integrity) - useful for modeling how trust develops
2. **Risk and vulnerability** - trust involves willingness to be vulnerable
3. **Propensity to trust** - individual differences in baseline trust levels
4. **Dynamic relationship** - trustworthiness perceptions lead to trust, which leads to risk-taking

**How simulation could use this correctly:**
- Model trust formation based on perceived ability, benevolence, and integrity of governments/AI systems
- Differentiate between trustworthiness (attributes) and trust (willingness to be vulnerable)
- Include propensity to trust as a population parameter

## Correct Sources for Trust Restoration

**If the wiki wants to cite research on trust restoration after violations, these are appropriate sources:**

### 1. Kim et al. (2006) - Trust Repair After Violations
**Citation:** Kim, P.H., Ferrin, D.L., Cooper, C.D., & Dirks, K.T. (2004). "Removing the shadow of suspicion: The effects of apology versus denial for repairing competence- versus integrity-based trust violations." *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 89(1), 104-118.

**Contribution:** Empirical study showing:
- Competence-based violations are easier to repair than integrity-based violations
- Apologies more effective for integrity violations, denials can work for competence violations
- Trust restoration depends on attribution of the violation

### 2. Dirks, Lewicki & Zaheer (2009) - Trust Repair Framework
**Citation:** Dirks, K.T., Lewicki, R.J., & Zaheer, A. (2009). "Repairing relationships within and between organizations: Building a conceptual foundation." *Academy of Management Review*, 34(1), 68-84.

**Contribution:** Comprehensive framework for trust repair including:
- Structural processes to discourage negative exchange
- Verbal strategies (apologies, explanations)
- Time dynamics in repair processes

### 3. Gillespie & Dietz (2009) - Four-Step Trust Repair
**Citation:** Gillespie, N., & Dietz, G. (2009). "Trust repair after an organization-level failure." *Academy of Management Review*, 34(1), 127-145.

**Contribution:** Four-step repair process:
1. Immediate response
2. Diagnosis (assess violation type)
3. Reforming (demonstrate change)
4. Evaluation (rebuild confidence)

### 4. Bottom et al. (2002) - Restoration of Cooperation
**Citation:** Bottom, W.P., Gibson, K., Daniels, S.E., & Murnighan, J.K. (2002). "When talk is not cheap: Substantive penance and expressions of intent in rebuilding cooperation." *Organization Science*, 13(5), 497-513.

**Contribution:** Empirical evidence that:
- **Substantive penance** (costly signals) more effective than cheap talk
- Trust restoration requires behavioral proof, not just verbal assurances
- Consistent positive signals needed over time (this might be where wiki's "Rousseau" claim comes from)

## Simulation Implications

**Current simulation mechanics (from wiki):**
- Trust degrades quickly on bad events (-0.1 to -0.2)
- Trust recovers slowly on good events (+0.05/month if QoL improving)
- Recovery slower than decay

**This is well-supported by:**
1. ✅ **Slovic (1993):** Asymmetry principle (verified)
2. ❌ **NOT Mayer et al. (1995):** Wrong citation
3. ⚠️ **Unclear if Rousseau et al. (1998):** Needs verification

**Better justification:**
1. **Slovic (1993):** Empirical asymmetry ratio (3-4x)
2. **Kim et al. (2004):** Violation type matters (distinguish competence vs. integrity violations in events)
3. **Bottom et al. (2002):** Substantive penance requirement (governments must take costly actions, not just promises)
4. **Dirks et al. (2009):** Time-dependent recovery (slow recovery is realistic)

**Suggested enhancement:**
Consider implementing:
- **Violation type detection:** Competence failures (policy mistakes) vs. integrity violations (corruption, lying)
- **Repair mechanism differentiation:** Apologies work for integrity, fixing problems works for competence
- **Substantive penance:** Costly government actions (e.g., budget reallocation to affected groups) have stronger repair effects than cheap talk
- **Time decay of repair:** Recent positive actions have stronger effect, old actions fade
