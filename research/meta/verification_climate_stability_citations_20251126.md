## Layer 2: Claim vs Content Verification

### 1. IPCC AR6 WG1 Ch4 - RCP8.5 to 2300

**Location:** Lines 414-415
**Claim:** "Worst-case scenarios (RCP8.5) show severe but not complete climate system collapse by 2300"

**Actual Content:**
- AR6 projects **6.6-14.1C warming by 2300** under SSP5-8.5 (equivalent to RCP8.5)
- Described as "a level not seen since the 'Early Eocene Climate' about 50m years ago"
- The specific phrase "severe but not complete climate system collapse" does NOT appear in AR6

**Verdict:** **UNSUPPORTED**

The claim paraphrases but the IPCC does not characterize outcomes as "not complete collapse." The 6.6-14.1C range represents unprecedented warming in human history. The IPCC emphasizes uncertainty and risk, not reassurance about stability.

**Severity:** SIGNIFICANT - The citation is real but the claim overstates what IPCC actually says

### 2. Armstrong McKay et al. (2022, Science) - Hothouse Earth

**Location:** Lines 416-417, 449-450
**Claim:** "Multiple tipping points crossing leads to 'Hothouse Earth' but not complete destabilization"

**Actual Content:**
From author explainer (climatetippingpoints.info):
> "most at this level don't substantially amplify global warming in the shorter term, and so won't trigger a 'runaway' climate change scenario"

From Carbon Brief analysis:
> "rising temperatures may have already pushed the planet beyond a 'safe climate state'"

Key finding: Paper says tipping points are "not a 'game over' situation" in that emissions cuts still matter, NOT that destabilization doesn't occur.

**Verdict:** **OVERSTATED**

The paper argues against "runaway" scenarios but does NOT claim Earth remains stable. It emphasizes that even with multiple tipping points, human emissions choices still determine future trajectory. This is about agency, not stability.

**Severity:** SIGNIFICANT - Conflates "not runaway" with "not destabilized"

### 3. Lenton et al. (2019, Nature) - Self-Limiting Feedbacks

**Location:** Lines 443-444, 468
**Claim:** "Even crossing multiple tipping points, Earth systems retain some stability through self-limiting feedbacks"

**Actual Content:**
From PIK summary of the paper:
> "we must admit that we have underestimated the risks of unleashing irreversible changes, where the planet **self-amplifies global warming**"

The paper's title is literally: "Climate tipping points - **too risky to bet against**"

The authors declare: "The evidence from tipping points alone suggests that we are in a **state of planetary emergency**."

The paper describes POSITIVE feedbacks (self-amplifying), NOT self-limiting feedbacks.

**Verdict:** **MISREPRESENTED (CRITICAL)**

This citation says the OPPOSITE of what is claimed. The paper emphasizes:
- RISK, not stability
- SELF-AMPLIFICATION, not self-limitation
- EMERGENCY, not resilience

**Severity:** CRITICAL - Direct contradiction of source material

### 4. Zachos et al. (2008, Nature) - PETM Recovery

**Location:** Lines 445-446
**Claim:** "After +5-8C spike, climate stabilized within ~200ky... demonstrating system resilience"

**Actual Content:**
- PETM warming: **4-5C global average** (surface ocean 5-8C is regional, not global)
- Duration of entire PETM event: **150-220ky** (not recovery time)
- Recovery time specifically: **~100ky** for carbon cycle (shorter than claimed)
- PETM caused "**largest deep-sea mass extinction** in 93 million years"
- Ocean acidification led to "complete dissolution of all CaCO3 shells"

**Verdict:** **OVERSTATED**

The temperature range is approximately correct but the framing as "demonstrating resilience" is problematic. The event caused mass extinction and catastrophic ocean acidification. Recovery occurred over geological time, which is not "resilience" in any policy-relevant sense.

**Severity:** SIGNIFICANT - Conflates "eventual geological recovery" with "system resilience"

### 5. Steffen et al. (2015, Science) - Planetary Boundaries

**Location:** Lines 447-448 (referencing 2018 paper, not 2015)
**Claim:** "Safe operating space may be exceeded but Earth remains habitable"

**Actual Content:**
From Stockholm Resilience Centre:
> "Transgression of the planetary boundaries creates substantial risk of destabilizing the Holocene state"

From Mongabay analysis:
> "Humanity is already existing outside the safe operating space for at least four of the nine boundaries"

The framework acknowledges transgressions occur without immediate collapse, but emphasizes INCREASING RISK, not guaranteed habitability.

**Verdict:** **VERIFIED (with caveats)**

The framework does distinguish between "inside safe operating space" and "high risk zone" - and we currently exist in transgression without immediate collapse. The claim is defensible.

**Severity:** MINOR - Accurate but could be more precise about risk framing

### 6. Royer (2006, GCA) - Phanerozoic Climate Stability

**Location:** Line context (implicit in 5% floor)
**Claim:** "No known Phanerozoic climate state was completely destabilized despite mass extinctions"

**Actual Content:**
The 2006 paper focuses on CO2-temperature correlations and climate thresholds, specifically:
- CO2 below ~500ppm correlates with glaciation
- CO2 below ~1000ppm correlates with cool conditions
- Tight CO2-temperature coupling over 540 million years

The paper does NOT make claims about "complete destabilization" or stability during mass extinctions. It's about CO2 forcing, not system resilience.

**Verdict:** **UNSUPPORTED**

The claim reads too much into a paper about CO2-temperature correlations. The paper does not discuss whether climate states were "destabilized" - it discusses correlation patterns.

**Severity:** MODERATE - Infers conclusions not present in source

### 7. Meadows et al. (1972) - Limits to Growth

**Location:** Lines 535-536
**Claim:** "Extreme pollution causes collapse, which reduces industrial output and emissions" (self-limiting)

**Actual Content:**
From Wikipedia summary:
> "the burst of pollution, caused by the burst of the industrial output per capita... eventually degrades land, causing a starvation that regulates the population"

The model does show pollution-induced collapse reducing industrial output - but this is catastrophic collapse, not a stabilizing mechanism.

**Verdict:** **VERIFIED**

The citation accurately represents the Limits to Growth model dynamics. Pollution IS self-limiting in the model - through catastrophic collapse. Whether this supports a [0,1] pollution bound is another question.

**Severity:** MINOR - Accurate citation, debatable application

## Final Notes

The mechanisms themselves (5% floor, 95% cap, [0,1] pollution bounds) are reasonable modeling choices. The problem is the citations were assembled to justify existing code rather than derived from rigorous literature review. This is a common pattern but leads to citation quality issues.

**Key insight:** "Earth eventually recovered from PETM" is true but misleading for policy. Recovery took 100-200ky and involved mass extinction. Using this to justify a 5% floor conflates geological resilience with human-relevant stability.

Better framing: "These bounds prevent modeling artifacts. Earth has survived worse but human civilization has not. Use simulation results to understand risks, not to assume safety margins exist."
