**15 Ferrets with Willa Genetics:**
- **Source:** Revive & Restore "Five Years Later" article (2025)
- **Direct Quote:** "Today, just five years after Elizabeth Ann's birth, **15 ferrets in the breeding population carry Willa's genetic material**, establishing her as the eighth founder and breaking the genetic bottleneck that had constrained the species for decades."

**Timeline Verification:**
- December 2020 (Elizabeth Ann birth) → 2025 (5 years later)
- ✅ **Exactly 5 years as claimed**

**Impact:**

## CLAIM 4: Economic Incentives (Vicuña 6K→350K, Buffalo hundreds→500K)

### 4A: Vicuña Population Recovery via Fiber Trade

**Original Claim:**
> "Vicuña 6K → 350K via fiber trade"

**Initial Population (1960s):**
- **Source:** Wikipedia (Vicuña), IUCN reports, peer-reviewed literature
- **Quote 1:** "Hunting of the vicuña was unrestricted until 1964, which reduced its numbers to only **6,000 in the 1960s**."
- **Quote 2:** "By 1960, the vicuña population had dropped from its pre-colonial population of 2 million to an estimated **10,000 individuals**."
- **Range:** 6,000-10,000 (both numbers appear in literature, 6K is the lower bound)

**Recovered Population (2008):**
- **Source:** Multiple conservation studies, IUCN Red List updates
- **Quote:** "Vicuña populations increased from 10,000 individuals in 1960 to **∼350,000 in 2008**, representing a remarkable recovery over several decades."

**Peru-Specific Data:**
- 1994: 66,559
- 1997: 103,161
- 2000: 118,678
- 2012: 208,899

**Economic Incentive Mechanism:**
- **Source:** IUCN case studies, CITES reports
- **Quote:** "The decision to grant usufructory rights to communities to shear and sell vicuña fibre increased their economic incentive to sustainably manage and protect the species."
- **Timeline:** Starting in 1980, Peru created legal markets for vicuña fibers
- **1994:** CITES lifted ban on international trade in vicuña fiber and garments

**IUCN Status Changes:**
- 1974: Listed as Endangered
- 2008: Reclassified as "Least Concern"

**Assessment:** ✅ **VERIFIED** - 6K→350K recovery via fiber trade economics substantiated
