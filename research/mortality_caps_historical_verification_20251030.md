## 🚨 CRITICAL: Holodomor Rate Interpretation

### Claim: "Regional excess mortality: 140 per 1,000 overall; >200 per 1,000 in worst oblasts"

**Status:** 🚨 **AMBIGUOUS - TIME UNIT UNCLEAR**

**Evidence Found:**
1. **Wolowyna et al. 2020 paper** (Cambridge Core, Nationalities Papers): Could not access full text to verify exact quote
2. **Harvard MAPA Digital Atlas**: States "133.3 per 1,000 population" for Soviet Ukraine 1932-1934, explicitly noting this is **for the entire 2-3 year period**, not annual
3. **Search results indicate**: "13.3% of Ukraine's population died during 1932-34" = 133 per 1,000 **cumulative**

**Cross-Reference with Other Famines:**

| Famine | Peak Annual Mortality | Peak Monthly Mortality | Total Cumulative |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|------------------|
| **Holodomor** (claimed) | **14-20%?** | **1.4-2%?** | 13.3% over 2-3 years |
| **Great Leap Forward** | 2.5% (1960) | ~0.2% | 15-55M total |
| **Bengal 1943** | ~5-6% | ~0.5-1% peak | 5-6% total |
| **Irish Potato** | ~6% (1847) | ~0.5% | 12-18% over 7 years |

**Inference from Pattern:**
- **Great Leap Forward peak**: 2.5% annual = 25 per 1,000 annual
- **Holodomor claim**: 140-200 per 1,000 = 14-20%
- **If annual**: This would be **5-8× worse** than Great Leap Forward (implausible given similar famine mechanisms)
- **If cumulative over 2-3 years**: 140-200 per 1,000 cumulative = 14-20% total deaths (plausible, matches other estimates)

**Peak Monthly Mortality (June 1933):**
- **Research file states**: 28,000 deaths/day × 30 = 840,000/month
- **Ukraine population**: ~30 million
- **Calculation**: 840,000/30,000,000 = **2.8% monthly** (this is clearly stated and consistent)

**Recommendation:**
The "140-200 per 1,000" is **almost certainly CUMULATIVE** over the 1932-1934 famine period, NOT annual. This aligns with:
- Harvard MAPA's "133.3 per 1,000 for 1932-1934"
- Peak monthly rate of 2.8% (which would be 33.6% if sustained for a year, but it wasn't)
- Total death toll of 3.9M out of 30M = 13% overall

**CORRECTED INTERPRETATION:**
- **Regional cumulative mortality (1932-1934)**: 140 per 1,000 overall (14%), >200 per 1,000 (20%) in worst oblasts
- **Peak monthly mortality (June 1933)**: 2.8% (28 per 1,000)
- **Annual rate would have been** (if sustained): ~30% - but famine was concentrated in 7 months
