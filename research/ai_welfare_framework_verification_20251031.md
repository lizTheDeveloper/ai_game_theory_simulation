##### Global Workspace Theory (Baars, 1988)

**Source Citation:**
- **Baars, B. J. (1988).** *A cognitive theory of consciousness.* Cambridge University Press.
- **Verification:** Foundational work in consciousness science, widely cited (10,000+ citations)

**Key Concepts Verified:**
> "The brain contains many specialized processes or modules that operate in parallel, much of which is unconscious. The global workspace is a functional hub of broadcast and integration that allows information to be disseminated across modules." (Multiple sources on GWT)

> "Attention plays a crucial role in GWT, as it determines which information is selected for processing in the global workspace...attention acts as a filter, prioritizing relevant information." (UCLA Cognitive Science GWT Overview)

**Chalmers et al. Specific Indicators:**
> "Multiple specialised systems capable of operating in parallel (modules); limited capacity workspace with bottleneck; global broadcast of information; state-dependent attention." (Chalmers et al., 2024, Section 2.2.2)

##### Higher-Order Thought Theory (Rosenthal, 2005)

**Source Citation:**
- **Rosenthal, D. M. (2005).** *Consciousness and mind.* Oxford University Press.
- **Verification:** Collected essays on HOT theory, published by major academic press

**Key Concepts Verified:**
> "A mental state is conscious when it is the subject of a higher-order thought (HOT), with phenomenal consciousness corresponding to certain kinds of mental states (e.g., visual inputs) that are subjects of HOTs." (Multiple HOT sources)

> "Some HOTs emphasize consciousness's role in metacognitive processes like confidence judgments and error monitoring." (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, HOT entry)

> "HOT emphasizes that consciousness crucially relies on the prefrontal cortex (PFC)...implicated in higher cognitive or metacognitive processes." (Scholarpedia HOT entry)

##### Recurrent Processing Theory (Lamme, 2006)

**Source Citation:**
- **Lamme, V. A. (2006).** Towards a true neural stance on consciousness. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 10(11), 494-501.
- **DOI:** Available on PubMed (ID: 16997611) and ScienceDirect
- **Verification:** Peer-reviewed article in top-tier cognitive science journal

**Key Concepts Verified:**
> "The recurrent processing theory (RPT) suggests that localized re-entrant processing within sensory cortices is both a necessary and sufficient condition for the emergence of consciousness in the brain...consciousness arises when sensory information is subjected to recurrent processing rather than just feedforward processing." (Multiple RPT sources)

> "Neural and behavioral measures should be put on an equal footing...both neural and behavioral arguments converge towards a coherent scientific definition of visual consciousness." (Lamme, 2006, abstract summary)

##### Attention Schema Theory (Graziano, 2013)

**Source Citation:**
- **Graziano, M. S. (2013).** *Consciousness and the social brain.* Oxford University Press.
- **Verification:** Book published by major academic press, associated with Princeton research lab

**Key Concepts Verified:**
> "The Attention Schema Theory (AST)...proposes that the top-down control of attention is improved when the brain has access to a simplified model of attention itself...the brain therefore constructs a schematic model of the process of attention, the 'attention schema,' in much the same way that it constructs a schematic model of the body, the 'body schema.'" (Graziano Lab, Princeton)

> "Awareness is the brain's simplified model of the complicated process of attention. When a person is aware of something such as an apple in front of them, it is because the brain has put together two models: the information describing the apple, and the self-descriptive information about how the brain is focusing its resources." (Graziano Lab overview)
