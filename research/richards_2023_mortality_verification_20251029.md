## Key Findings from Paper

### Mortality Projection
**Verified claim:** The simulation indicates approximately **6 billion deaths due to starvation by 2100**

- Timeframe: 2025-2100 (75 years) ✅
- Mechanism: Food insecurity and starvation (not direct climate deaths)
- Distribution: "Unequal distribution" of mortality across regions

### Scenario Characteristics

**Critical Context - This is an EXTREME scenario, NOT baseline:**

1. **Temperature increase:** 8-12°C+ by 2100
   - This is 4-6× higher than current trajectory (2.0-4.9°C)
   - Requires rapid combustion of ALL available fossil fuels
   - Represents worst-case, low-probability scenario

2. **Explicitly described as:**
   - "Runaway global warming scenario" (not baseline)
   - "Artificial scenario" (authors' own characterization)
   - "Understudied compared to low- to mid-range scenarios"
   - "High-range global warming" (extreme case)

3. **Based on:**
   - World3 model's runaway scenario
   - Accelerated high-resolution SSP5-RCP8.5 data
   - SSP5-RCP8.5 is itself an extreme scenario (>90% of no-policy baselines produce lower emissions)

### Research Purpose
The paper explicitly states its purpose is to explore **understudied extreme scenarios** to inform adaptation and resilience strategies, NOT to predict the most likely outcome.

## Critical Issue: Mischaracterization in Wiki

**Wiki statement:** "baseline climate collapse scenario"
**Actual characterization:** Extreme/artificial/runaway scenario

**Why this matters:**

1. **Misleading comparison:** The wiki uses this as a benchmark to justify the simulation's "7.76 billion deaths over 30 years," but compares an accelerated simulation to what the wiki calls "baseline" when it's actually an extreme scenario

2. **Compression justification problem:** The wiki states the simulation is "2.5× faster than published climate models" by comparing:
   - Simulation: 30 years, 7.76 billion deaths
   - Richards et al.: 75 years, 6 billion deaths
   - This creates false equivalence: "Magnitude comparable, timeline compressed"

3. **Scientific rigor:** Using an extreme scenario as a "baseline" comparison fundamentally misrepresents the state of climate science

## What the Paper Actually Shows

### Paper's Explicit Caveats

1. **Current trajectories:** 2.0-4.9°C by 2100 (NOT 8-12°C)
2. **Scenario probability:** Requires unrealistic coal expansion (6.5× current use by 2100)
3. **Purpose:** Explore tail risks, not predict central outcomes
4. **Authors emphasize:** This is an "artificial" scenario for studying extreme risks

### Proper Context for Use

**How Richards et al. 2023 SHOULD be cited:**

✅ Correct: "Richards et al. (2023) model an **extreme runaway warming scenario** (8-12°C by 2100) showing ~6 billion deaths from starvation, representing a worst-case tail risk"

❌ Incorrect (current wiki): "Richards et al. (2023): 6 billion deaths over 75 years (**baseline climate collapse scenario**)"

## Recommendations

### 1. Immediate Wiki Correction Required

**Current text (line 1140):**
```
- **Richards et al. (2023):** 6 billion deaths over 75 years (baseline climate collapse scenario)
```

**Should be:**
```
- **Richards et al. (2023):** ~6 billion deaths over 75 years (extreme runaway warming scenario: 8-12°C by 2100, artificial scenario for studying tail risks)
```

### 2. Reconsider Simulation Justification

The current comparison:
- Richards: 6B deaths / 75 years = 80M deaths/year (extreme scenario)
- Simulation: 7.76B deaths / 30 years = 259M deaths/year (3.2× higher rate)

**Problems:**
1. Comparing to an extreme scenario as if it's baseline
2. Simulation runs 3× faster AND reaches higher total mortality
3. Need better justification for why simulation should exceed even extreme scenarios

**Better approach:**
- Compare to mainstream IPCC scenarios (AR6 projections)
- Explicitly justify if modeling beyond Richards et al.'s extreme case
- Cite additional research supporting rapid cascade scenarios

### 3. Additional Research Needed

To properly contextualize the simulation's mortality projections, need:

1. **Mainstream projections:** IPCC AR6 mortality estimates under 2-4°C scenarios
2. **Cascade dynamics:** Research on multi-tipping-point interactions (Lenton et al. cited but need verification)
3. **Food system collapse timescales:** Is 30-year collapse plausible? What mechanisms support it?
4. **Adaptation factors:** How much do emergency responses typically reduce mortality in historical crises?

## Additional Notes

### Paper's Key Contributions

The Richards et al. paper makes valuable contributions despite (or because of) its extreme scenario focus:

1. **Identifies research gap:** High-range warming understudied
2. **Food system vulnerability:** Modern globalized systems uniquely fragile
3. **Distributional analysis:** Shows inequality in mortality burden
4. **Methodological advance:** High-resolution World3 implementation

### Why Extreme Scenarios Matter

While not predictions, extreme scenarios serve important functions:
- Identify vulnerabilities in current systems
- Test limits of adaptation strategies
- Motivate precautionary action
- Explore "deep uncertainty" in climate system

**But:** They must be clearly labeled as such, not presented as baseline expectations.
