## Executive Summary

Marine Ice Sheet Instability (MISI/MICI) represents a critical tail-risk mechanism for abrupt sea level rise events (1-3m jumps) driven by Antarctic and Greenland ice sheet dynamics. While debate continues about timing and magnitude, recent 2024 research confirms the physical mechanisms are real but less extreme than 2016 projections suggested.

**Key Parameters Extracted:**
- **Temperature thresholds:** 2-3°C subsurface ocean warming triggers WAIS collapse
- **Abrupt event magnitude:** 0.5-3m sea level rise per event (probability distribution required)
- **Timescale:** Decades to century for event completion (not instantaneous)
- **Irreversibility:** Hysteresis behavior confirmed (aligns with M-7 climate irreversibility)
- **Economic impact:** $14T/year by 2100 for all sea level rise adaptation costs
- **Population displacement:** 13M in US alone, 2-110M in Bangladesh per meter of rise

## Physical Mechanisms

### Marine Ice Sheet Instability (MISI)
**Physics:** Occurs when bedrock beneath grounding line has reverse slope (deepens toward ice sheet interior)
- Small retreat of grounding line onto deeper bed → greater ice flux
- Positive flux feedback → rapid and irreversible retreat
- **This is the baseline mechanism (distinct from MICI)**

**Sources:**
- [Marine Ice Sheet Instability → Term](https://climate.sustainability-directory.com/term/marine-ice-sheet-instability/)
- [Marine Ice Sheet instability](https://www.antarcticglaciers.org/antarctica-2/west-antarctic-ice-sheet-2/marine-ice-sheets/)

### Marine Ice Cliff Instability (MICI)
**Physics:** Structural collapse of tall ice cliffs (>100m) under their own weight after ice shelf buttressing is lost
- Requires hydrofracturing of ice shelves (atmospheric warming driven)
- Ice cliff heights exceed structural failure threshold
- **More speculative than MISI, contested in literature**

### Subsurface Ocean Warming (Primary Trigger)
**Temperature Thresholds:**
- **2-3°C subsurface warming** → potential WAIS collapse (Antarctic modeling studies)
- **1°C increase** in ocean temperatures → up to **10 meters per year** of basal melting at grounding zone
- **3°C subsurface warming** used in Last Interglacial simulations (7.5m sea level equivalent)

**Mechanism:**
- Subsurface ocean warming (100-500m depth) drives ice shelf basal melt
- Strongest warming projected in Ross Sea: 2-4°C warmer by 2120s at 400m depth
- Accounting for Antarctic discharge raises subsurface ocean temperatures by >1°C at ice margin

**Grounding Zone Vulnerability:**
- **Tipping point behavior:** Ocean water intrusion at grounding zone can lead to runaway melting
- Ice sheets more vulnerable than previously thought to this mechanism (2024 Nature Geoscience)

**Confidence Level:** HIGH for mechanism, MEDIUM for specific thresholds

**Sources:**
- [East Antarctic warming forced by ice loss during the Last Interglacial | Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-45501-x)

## Recommended Parameters for Simulation

### Sea Level Rise Mechanics
**Baseline (gradual):**
- Current rate: 3.4mm/year (accelerating)
- Acceleration factor: 0.1-0.2mm/year² based on warming trajectory
- Source: Thermal expansion + mountain glaciers + baseline ice sheet melt

**Abrupt Events (MICI - tail risk):**
- **Trigger:** Subsurface ocean temperature >2.5°C above pre-industrial (Ross Sea, Amundsen Sea)
- **Probability per decade:**
  - Before 2100: 1-5% (if trigger reached)
  - 2100-2200: 5-15% (if trigger reached)
  - After 2200: 15-30% (if trigger reached)
- **Magnitude distribution:** Log-normal with median=1m, σ=0.5m, range=[0.5m, 3m]
- **Duration:** 20-50 years for event to complete (not instantaneous)
- **Irreversibility:** Once triggered, requires >500 years and significant cooling to reverse (hysteresis)

### Impact Propagation (per meter of sea level rise)
**Coastal population displacement:**
- **Formula:** DisplacedPop = CoastalPop × ExposureFraction(elevation)
- **Exposure fractions:**
  - 0-1m elevation: 100% displaced
  - 1-2m elevation: 80% displaced
  - 2-5m elevation: 40% displaced
  - 5-10m elevation: 10% displaced
- **Global coastal population:** ~2.4 billion (2020)

**Infrastructure damage costs:**
- **Per meter rise:** $500B-1T globally (one-time capital costs)
- **Annual adaptation costs:** Scale with cumulative rise (linear to quadratic relationship)
- **Regional variation:** Developed nations 60% armoring, developing nations more displacement

**Agricultural land loss:**
- **Per meter rise:** ~17,500 km² land (50% agricultural)
- **Food production impact:** -0.5% to -2% depending on region (delta regions most vulnerable)

**Economic impacts:**
- **GDP shock:** -0.1% per meter of abrupt rise (uncertainty shock + capital destruction)
- **Adaptation expenditure:** 0.5-2% GDP/year depending on commitment to protection vs retreat

### Feedback Loops
**Climate-MICI coupling:**
- Antarctic meltwater → subsurface ocean warming (+1-2°C Ross Sea feedback)
- Ice loss → albedo feedback (minor for Antarctica, sea ice already present)
- AMOC slowdown from meltwater → regional sea level rise amplification (+20-30% North Atlantic)

**Economic-migration coupling:**
- Mass displacement → social instability (migration crisis mechanics from other systems)
- Infrastructure loss → reduced adaptation capacity (negative spiral)

**Irreversible:** Once triggered, cannot reverse even with aggressive cooling (hysteresis trap)
