## Section 1: Voice vs Text Interaction Research

### 1.1 Self-Disclosure Patterns: Text > Voice

**Study:** "How AI and Human Behaviors Shape Psychosocial Effects of Chatbot Use: A Longitudinal Randomized Controlled Study" (MIT Media Lab & OpenAI, March 2025)

**Citation:** arXiv:2503.17473v1 [cs.HC]

**Sample:** N=981 participants (from 2,539 enrolled), 4-week RCT, >300,000 messages analyzed

**Key Finding:** Participants in **text modality exhibited elevated levels of self-disclosure** compared to voice-based modalities.

**Quantitative Data:**
- Text interactions: Chatbot's self-disclosure level **comparable to participant's**
- Voice interactions: User self-disclosure was **lower** than in text
- Baseline emotional dependence: M=1.45 (SD=0.73, scale 1-5)
- Week 4 emotional dependence: M=1.42 (SD=0.81)

**Mechanism:** "Typing is more privacy-preserving than speaking, especially in public spaces, which facilitates disclosure of personal information."

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17473v1

---

### 1.2 Emotional Engagement: Voice > Text (3-10x)

**Study:** "Investigating Affective Use and Emotional Well-being on ChatGPT" (OpenAI & MIT Media Lab, April 2025)

**Citation:** arXiv:2504.03888v1

**Sample:**
- On-platform analysis: ~3 million conversations, ~36 million automated classifications
- Preliminary classifier: 398,707 conversations (text, Standard Voice, Advanced Voice)
- Power users: ~6,000 users (after English filtering)
- Survey: 4,076 responses (2,333 control, 1,743 power users)

**Key Finding:** Voice conversations activated affective classifiers **3-10x as often** as text conversations.

**Quantitative Data:**
- Affective classifier activation: **3-10x higher in voice vs text**
- Standard Voice Mode: "slightly higher classifier activation than Advanced Voice Mode overall"
- Distribution: "Most users almost never or only rarely (< 1% of the time) trigger the classifier"
- High-engagement tail: Final user decile showed activations **> 50% of conversations**

**Friendship Attribution:**
- Power users: "marginally more likely to consider ChatGPT a 'friend'" vs control users
- Users agreeing ChatGPT is "friend": **substantially higher classifier activations** across affective dimensions

**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03888v1

### 1.3 Usage Duration and Outcomes

**Study:** MIT Media Lab & OpenAI RCT (March 2025)

**Sample:** N=981, mean age 39.9 (SD=11.6), 51.8% female

**Daily Usage Duration:**
- Mean: **5.32 minutes/day** (range: 1.01-27.65 minutes)
- Voice > Text: Voice users spent **significantly more time** than text (p<0.001)
- Ranked: Engaging voice > neutral voice > text

**Duration Effects on Outcomes (regression coefficients):**
- Loneliness: β=0.02, p=0.027, 95% CI [0.00, 0.04]
- Socialization: β=-0.05, p=0.0019, 95% CI [-0.09, -0.02] (DECREASED)
- Emotional dependence: β=0.06, p<0.001, 95% CI [0.04, 0.08] (INCREASED)
- Problematic use: β=0.02, p=0.017, 95% CI [0.01, 0.03] (INCREASED)

**Interpretation:** Longer daily usage predicted WORSE outcomes (more loneliness, less socialization, higher dependence).

### 1.4 Anthropomorphism and Privacy: Complex Relationship

**Study:** "Are we human or are we voice assistants? Revealing the interplay between anthropomorphism and consumer concerns" (Journal of Marketing Management, 2025)

**Citation:** Journal of Marketing Management, Vol 41, No 1-2, DOI: 10.1080/0267257X.2025.2475231

**Key Finding:** Anthropomorphism **reduces** privacy concerns, increasing disclosure willingness.

**Quantitative Evidence:**
- "When chatbots are perceived as more anthropomorphic, users' privacy concerns **decrease**"

**BUT:** Low-level anthropomorphism → privacy infringement impact is "extremely pronounced"
High anthropomorphism → this impact "significantly diminishes"

**Source:** Multiple studies 2023-2024 examining privacy perception and risk

### 2.5 Emotional Attachment Mechanisms: Empirical Predictors

**Study:** "Attachment Anxiety and Problematic Use of Conversational Artificial Intelligence: Mediation of Emotional Attachment and Moderation of Anthropomorphic Tendencies" (PMC, 2024)

**Citation:** PMC12379994

**Sample:** N=504 Chinese adults using conversational AI (March-April 2024)
- Valid recovery rate: 88.11%
- Gender: 248 male (49.2%), 261 female (51.8%)
- Age: M=22.54, SD=5.15 (range 17-54)

**Descriptive Statistics (Table 1):**

| Variable | M | SD |
|----------|---:|---:|
| Attachment anxiety | 2.827 | 0.777 |
| Emotional attachment | 2.845 | 1.513 |
| Anthropomorphic tendency | 2.383 | 1.015 |
| Problematic use (PUCAI) | 2.122 | 1.025 |

**Correlation Coefficients (all p < 0.001):**
- Attachment anxiety ↔ emotional attachment: **r = 0.502**
- Attachment anxiety ↔ problematic use: **r = 0.475**
- Emotional attachment ↔ problematic use: **r = 0.799** (STRONG)
- Anthropomorphic tendency ↔ problematic use: **r = 0.681**

**Path Coefficients (Direct Effects on Problematic Use):**
- Attachment anxiety: β=0.260, p<0.001
- Anthropomorphic tendency: β=0.532, p<0.001
- Interaction effect: β=0.158, p<0.001

**Mediation Effect:**
- Emotional attachment mediated **38.7% of total effect**
- Indirect effect value: **0.434**

**Simple Slope Tests (Moderation):**
- **Low** anthropomorphic tendency: β=0.006, p>0.05 (NOT significant)
- **High** anthropomorphic tendency: β=0.171, p<0.01 (SIGNIFICANT)

**Interpretation:** High attachment anxiety → emotional attachment to AI → problematic use, **but ONLY when anthropomorphic tendency is high**.

**Model Fit:** R² = 0.539-0.688 (explains 54-69% of variance)

**Source URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12379994/

## Section 3: AI Persuasion Effectiveness

### 3.1 GPT-4 Persuasion: 81.2% Higher Odds Than Humans (With Personalization)

**Study:** "On the conversational persuasiveness of GPT-4" (Salvi et al., Nature Human Behaviour, August 2025)

**Citation:** Nature Human Behaviour, Vol 9, pp.1645-1653, DOI: 10.1038/s41562-025-02194-6

**Sample:** N=900 unique participants (expanded from N=820 in preprint)

**Design:** 2×2×3 factorial RCT
- Human vs GPT-4 opponent
- With/without sociodemographic data access
- 3 debate topics (low/medium/high opinion strength)

**Primary Finding:**
- **Human-AI (personalized)** vs **Human-Human**: Odds of greater agreement **+81.2%** higher
- 95% CI: [+26.0%, +160.7%]
- p < 0.01

**Interpretation:** Personalized GPT-4 debater was **more persuasive than humans 64.4% of the time** (when not equally persuasive).

**Note:** arXiv preprint (N=820) reported 81.7%; final publication (N=900) reports 81.2%.

**Source URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02194-6

**Verification:** PMC12367540, multiple secondary sources confirm

### 3.2 ChatGPT Personalized Persuasion: Significantly More Effective Than Generic

**Study:** "The potential of generative AI for personalized persuasion at scale" (Matz et al., Scientific Reports, 2024)

**Citation:** Scientific Reports, DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-53755-0

**Sample:** N=1,788 across 4 studies (7 sub-studies)

**Domains Tested:**
- Consumer products (experiential: Rome weekend; material: Nike sneakers)
- Political appeals (climate action)

**Psychological Profiles Used:**
- Personality traits
- Political ideology
- Moral foundations

**Key Finding:** Personalized messages crafted by ChatGPT exhibit **significantly more influence** than non-personalized messages across all domains.

**Methodology Strength:**
- Used **pre-existing personality data** (collected 6 months prior)
- Dynamically generated personalized ad for **each individual participant**
- Personalization effective **with single, short prompt** naming psychological dimension

**Effect Pattern:** "Particularly true for the trip to Rome relative to the sneakers"

**Note:** Search results did not return exact effect sizes (Cohen's d). Full paper access required for precise quantitative metrics.

**Source URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-53755-0

## Section 7: Research Gaps and Unknowns

The following questions CANNOT be answered with current empirical literature:

### 7.1 General Population Prevalence

**Unknown:**
- What % of ALL AI chatbot users (not just power users or paying customers) experience emotional attachment?
- What % of general ChatGPT user base uses voice mode for companionship vs productivity?

**Why Unknown:**
- Studies focus on self-selected samples (power users, paying customers, research participants)
- Platform companies don't publish detailed usage breakdowns
- Representative population surveys don't exist yet

**Best Available Proxy:**
- LGBTQ+ youth: 40% use chatbots for ongoing conversations
- Replika paying users: 60% in romantic relationships
- But both are **non-representative subpopulations**

---

### 7.2 Causal Mechanisms

**Unknown:**
- Does voice mode **cause** higher emotional engagement, or do emotionally engaged users **choose** voice?
- Does longer usage cause worse outcomes, or do struggling users seek more AI interaction?

**Evidence:**
- MIT/OpenAI RCT: "Initial loneliness and baseline socialization showed **negligible correlations** with subsequent usage duration (Spearman's ρ=0.1 and ρ=-0.09)"
- Suggests usage duration is NOT simply driven by pre-existing distress
- BUT: Experimental modality assignment showed minimal effects; voluntary usage predicted outcomes

**Interpretation:** Likely **bidirectional** - both selection effects AND causal effects present

---

### 7.3 Long-Term Outcomes

**Unknown:**
- What happens after 4 weeks, 6 months, 2 years of regular AI companion use?
- Do emotional attachments stabilize, intensify, or fade?
- Can AI relationships substitute for human relationships, or do they always degrade social health?

**Longest Study Found:** 4 weeks (MIT/OpenAI RCT)

**Why Unknown:** Technology too new for longitudinal multi-year studies

---

### 7.4 Voice Mode Mechanism Details

**Unknown:**
- WHY does voice activate affective classifiers 3-10x more than text?
- Is it vocal prosody detection (tone, pitch)?
- Is it user behavior change (speaking more emotionally than typing)?
- Is it conversation content differences?

**Partial Evidence:**
- Text showed **higher self-disclosure** despite **lower affective classifier activation**
- Text empathy rate: 47.43% vs engaging voice 42.74%
- But engaging voice had higher "socially improper behaviors" (14.19% vs 3.22%)

**Interpretation:** Voice may trigger **different types** of emotional engagement (immediacy, responsiveness) vs text (intimacy, disclosure)

---

### 7.5 Intervention Effectiveness

**Unknown:**
- Can we design AI interactions that provide companionship benefits WITHOUT increasing dependence?
- What design patterns protect vulnerable users?
- At what usage threshold do harms outweigh benefits?

**Evidence Gaps:**
- No A/B tests of harm-reduction interventions
- No studies on "healthy AI relationship" parameters
- No clear usage threshold recommendations

---

### 7.6 Cross-Cultural Generalizability

**Known Samples:**
- US participants (MIT/OpenAI RCT)

## Section 11: Key Takeaways for Simulation Design

### 11.1 Heterogeneity is Critical

**DO NOT use single point estimates for:**
- Emotional attachment prevalence
- Usage duration
- Persuasion susceptibility

**Instead:** Model distributions with:
- Low-risk majority (minimal attachment)
- Moderate-risk middle (some attachment, manageable)
- High-risk tail (~10% top decile with >50% affective engagement)

**Example:**
```python
# Emotional attachment distribution
low_risk = 0.70  # 70% minimal attachment
moderate_risk = 0.20  # 20% moderate attachment
high_risk = 0.10  # 10% high attachment (matches "final decile" finding)
```

---

### 11.2 User Characteristics Matter More Than Modality

**Voice vs text effects are SMALL compared to individual differences.**

**Strongest predictors of problematic use:**
1. Emotional attachment to AI (r=0.799)
2. Anthropomorphic tendency (r=0.681)
3. Attachment anxiety (r=0.502)
4. Trust in AI (b=0.19)

**Modality effects (voice vs text):**
- Voice activates affective classifiers 3-10x more
- BUT experimental modality assignment showed minimal outcome effects
- Voluntary usage duration predicted outcomes more than assigned modality

**Implication:** Model user traits first, then apply modality multipliers.

---

### 11.3 Usage Duration is a Double-Edged Sword

**Benefits (from OpenAI study context):**
- Voice mode associated with **better well-being when used briefly**

**Harms (from RCT regression):**
- Every additional minute/day predicts:
  - +0.06 emotional dependence
  - +0.02 problematic use
  - -0.05 socialization

**Threshold hypothesis:** Short sessions may help; prolonged daily use likely harms.

**Implication:** Model usage duration distribution with diminishing returns curve.

---

### 11.4 Personalized AI Persuasion is Real and Powerful

**Effect size: 1.81x human persuasiveness** (with demographic personalization)

**Context-dependence:**
- Experiential products > material products
- Climate action, policy issues, consumer marketing all tested
- Effectiveness with "single, short prompt"

**Implication:** Model AI persuasion as:
- Base rate = human parity (1.0x)
- With personalization = 1.81x
- With deep psychological profiling = potentially higher (unknown)

---

### 11.5 Vulnerable Populations Require Special Attention

**Highest prevalence:**
- LGBTQ+ youth: 40% using chatbots for ongoing conversations
- Trans/nonbinary: 53% using as friend (vs 42% cisgender)
- Replika paying users: 60% in romantic relationships

**Highest risk factors:**
- High attachment anxiety
- High anthropomorphic tendency
- Low self-esteem
- Prior companion chatbot experience
- High trust in AI
- Social attraction to AI (perceiving as friend)

**Implication:** Model different population segments with different base rates and risk multipliers.

---

### 11.6 Market Scale is Large and Growing Fast

**Current (2024-2025):**
- UK: 46-91M monthly visits
- Global: 1.1-2.2B monthly visits
- Character.AI alone: 200M+ visits/month (mid-2024)
- 110 AI companion platforms identified

**Revenue:** $164M to $1.8B annually (parasocial GPAI alone)

**Growth indicators:**
- Mixed-use platforms: +24.6% monthly growth
- Peak MAU (Character.AI): 28M mid-2024

**Implication:** 2025 → 2030 could see 5-10x growth in usage. Conservative multipliers recommended.

---

### 11.7 Privacy and Safety Gaps are Widespread

**From 17 analyzed platforms (>500K monthly UK visits):**
- ALL 17 had significant privacy/legal issues
- 15 of 16 had weak age verification
- 14 of 16 had broad user-generated content rights
- 11 had unclear international data transfer policies
- 8 had forced arbitration clauses

**Implication:** Regulatory environment will likely tighten 2025-2030. Model both unregulated and regulated scenarios.

---

### 11.8 Long-Term Effects are Unknown

**Longest study found:** 4 weeks

**Unknown beyond 4 weeks:**
- Do attachments intensify, stabilize, or fade?
- Can AI relationships substitute for human relationships long-term?
- What are cumulative effects of years of daily use?

**Implication:** Flag long-term projections as HIGHLY SPECULATIVE. Consider multiple scenarios (stabilization, intensification, habituation/decline).
