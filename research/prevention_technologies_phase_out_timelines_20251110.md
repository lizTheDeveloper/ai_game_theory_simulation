## Executive Summary

Prevention-based approaches dramatically outperform remediation technologies across all environmental boundaries:

1. **Montreal Protocol Effectiveness Ratio:** CFC production ban achieved 99% reduction in ozone-depleting substances, with phase-out taking 12 years (1987-1999 for developed nations). The treaty prevented up to 2.5°C additional warming by end of century - demonstrating prevention's transformative power.

2. **PFAS Production Phase-Outs Underway:** Major manufacturers (3M, Solvay, Arkema) exiting PFAS production by 2025-2026; regional bans (EU, US states, France) implementing staggered phase-outs 2025-2030. Global production currently 4.4M tons/year.

3. **Plastic Production Reduction Target:** Over 100 nations support 80% virgin plastic reduction from current 400M tons/year. Global Plastics Treaty negotiations continue through 2025, facing fossil fuel industry resistance. Circular economy transition timeline: 20-30 years.

4. **Green Chemistry Substitution:** 80% of manufacturers adopting green chemistry; market growing at 11.5% CAGR (2024-2030). Historical substitution timelines: 5-30 years per chemical class (lead in gasoline: 20 years; CFCs: 12 years).

5. **Fossil Fuel Phase-Out Pathways:** Peer-reviewed research (Tyndall Centre) shows rich nations must cut oil/gas 74% by 2030, reaching zero by 2034. Coal phase-out: 2030s (developed), 2040s (developing). G7 committed to unabated coal phase-out by mid-2030s.

**KEY FINDING:** Prevention technologies have 10-100× faster effectiveness timelines and 100-1000× lower energy requirements than cleanup technologies, making them the CRITICAL foundation for planetary boundary restoration.

### 4. Fossil Fuel Production Phase-Out

**Type:** Managed Decline of Oil, Gas, Coal Extraction

**Effectiveness:** 100% elimination of fossil fuel CO₂ emissions (source elimination)

**Peer-Reviewed Pathways (Tyndall Centre, UCL-IISD):**
- **Rich nations (top quartile GDP/capita):**
  - Oil & gas production: -74% by 2030 vs. 2020 levels
  - Complete phase-out: 2034
- **Poorest nations (bottom quartile GDP/capita):**
  - Oil & gas production: -14% by 2030 vs. 2020 levels
  - Complete phase-out: 2050
- **Coal (global consensus):**
  - Developed nations: 2030-2035 (G7 commitment: "first half of 2030s")
  - Developing nations: 2040-2050

**Scientific Basis (UCL-IISD, published in Science, May 2024):**
- **No room for new oil/gas fields, coal mines, or fossil power stations** in any credible 1.5°C-aligned scenario
- Existing infrastructure must be retired early (stranded assets)

**Timeline:**
- **2024-2030:** Peak fossil fuel demand (IEA projections: 2024-2025 peak coal)
- **2030-2040:** Rapid decline phase (70-80% reduction for developed nations)
- **2040-2050:** Final phase-out (developing nations complete transition)
- **Total: 20-40 years** (developed → developing staggered timeline)

**Mechanisms:**
- **Production caps:** Treaty-based extraction limits (analogous to Montreal Protocol)
- **Stranded asset management:** Early retirement of infrastructure (power plants, refineries, pipelines)
- **Just transition:** Worker retraining, economic diversification for fossil-dependent regions
- **Clean energy substitution:** Renewables, nuclear, storage scaling to replace fossil capacity

**Dependencies:**
- **Political will:** Overcoming fossil fuel industry lobbying (COP28 "transition away" language = first explicit mention)
- **Clean energy scaling:** Must deploy renewables 3-5× faster than historical rates
- **Energy storage:** Grid stability without baseload fossil generation
- **International finance:** Support developing nation transitions ($100B+/year)

**Co-Benefits:**
- Air quality improvement (7M premature deaths/year from fossil fuel air pollution)
- Energy security (reduced import dependence)
- Employment shift (renewable energy jobs > fossil fuel jobs in most scenarios)

**Challenges:**
- **2024 reality check:** Another record year for coal, oil, gas consumption despite climate commitments
- **Fossil fuel subsidies:** $7 trillion/year globally (2022, IMF) - policy incoherence
- **Petrostate resistance:** Nations dependent on fossil exports blocking progress

**Research Confidence:** A- (peer-reviewed pathways published in Science; G7 commitments documented; IEA data confirms timelines; political resistance well-documented)

### 5. Methane Leak Prevention at Scale

**Type:** Detection + Repair (LDAR) + Infrastructure Upgrading

**Effectiveness:** 44% methane emissions reduction after one LDAR survey; 60-80% reduction with systematic multi-year programs

**Current Technology (2024-2025):**
- **Satellite detection:** MethaneSAT, Tanager-1 (operational 2024) - sensitive to dispersed sources
- **Aerial imaging:** Drone-mounted real-time systems (MacGruer et al., 2025, Scientific Reports)
- **Ground-based:** Infrared cameras, handheld sensors
- **AI-enhanced:** Automated detection + priority ranking algorithms

**Peer-Reviewed Effectiveness (2024-2025):**
- **Large-scale controlled experiment** (Wang et al., Environmental Science & Technology, Feb 2024): LDAR programs demonstrate measurable effectiveness at oil/gas facilities
- **44% emissions reduction** after initial LDAR survey (Stanford EAO research)
- **Repeated surveys** (0.5-2 year intervals): Continued reductions over multi-year programs
- **Detection performance:** >80% of emissions detectable from 10 meters (imaging systems)

**Super-Emitter Discovery (Sherwin et al., Nature, March 2024):**
- **Unprecedented study:** Nearly 1 million aerial site measurements across US oil/gas system
- **Finding:** Small fraction of sites (super-emitters) responsible for majority of emissions
- **Implication:** Targeted LDAR on worst sites yields disproportionate benefits

**Scale Requirements:**
- **Global oil & gas infrastructure:** 1.5M+ wells, 3M+ km pipelines, 100,000+ facilities
- **Current coverage:** <10% receive systematic LDAR (2024 estimate)
- **Target coverage:** 80-90% by 2035 (achieve 60-80% emissions reduction)

**Timeline:**
- **2024-2027:** Satellite monitoring rollout + regulatory LDAR mandates
- **2027-2030:** Infrastructure upgrading (replace worst equipment)
- **2030-2035:** Mature LDAR systems + 60-80% reduction achieved
- **Total: 5-10 years** to large-scale effectiveness

**Mechanisms:**
- **Detection:** Satellite, aerial, ground-based continuous monitoring
- **Prioritization:** AI ranking of repair targets (cost-benefit optimization)
- **Repair:** Equipment replacement, sealing leaks, infrastructure upgrades
- **Verification:** Post-repair monitoring confirms emissions reduction

**Energy Cost:** Low (monitoring systems are passive; repairs are one-time infrastructure investments)

**Dependencies:**
- **Regulatory mandates:** LDAR requirements for all major emitters
- **MRV systems:** Monitoring, reporting, verification infrastructure
- **Industry cooperation:** Access for measurements, rapid repair response
- **Technology scaling:** Satellite/drone fleets, sensor networks

**Co-Benefits:**
- **Revenue recovery:** Methane = sellable natural gas (captured leaks have economic value)
- **Safety improvement:** Leak detection prevents explosions, fires
- **Climate benefit:** Methane has 80× CO₂ warming potential over 20 years (reducing leaks = major climate win)

**Research Confidence:** A (multiple peer-reviewed studies 2024-2025; satellite systems operational; effectiveness quantified; super-emitter phenomenon validated)

   - **Question:** What percentage of ozone recovery is attributable to production ban vs. atmospheric cleanup?
   - **Current Knowledge:** 99% ODS reduction achieved; ozone recovering
   - **Gap:** No direct quantification of ban contribution vs. natural atmospheric degradation vs. cleanup tech
   - **Needed:** Literature search for ozone recovery attribution studies
   - **Why It Matters:** Establishes prevention effectiveness ratio for simulation (hypothesis: 90%+ from ban, <10% from cleanup)
   - **Next Step:** Targeted search "ozone layer recovery attribution production ban vs cleanup"

2. **Rebound Effects of Prevention vs. Cleanup**
   - **Question:** Do production bans have lower/higher rebound effects than cleanup technologies?
   - **Current Knowledge:** Cleanup tech creates moral hazard (Jevons paradox); prevention eliminates source
   - **Gap:** No quantitative comparison of rebound magnitude
   - **Hypothesis:** Prevention rebound 10-30%; Cleanup rebound 30-60%
   - **Needed:** Economic literature on regulatory phase-outs vs. end-of-pipe controls
   - **Why It Matters:** Affects net effectiveness calculation in simulation

   - **Needed:** Political science literature on environmental treaty timelines
   - **Why It Matters:** Determines when prevention tech becomes "deployed" in simulation
