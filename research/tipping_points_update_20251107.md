**Contributors:** 100+ scientists from 20+ countries
**URL:** https://global-tipping-points.org/

**Key Finding:**
> "The planet has entered a 'new reality' as it hits its first climate tipping point."

**Coral Reef Tipping Point Crossed:**
- **Current warming:** ~1.4°C above pre-industrial
- **Coral thermal tipping point:** ~1.2°C
- **Status:** ALREADY EXCEEDED
- **Prognosis:** Even if warming stabilized at 1.5°C, reefs would likely continue to collapse

### Timescale Evidence

**Historical Coral Decline:**
- **1980s-1990s:** Gradual degradation begins
- **1998:** First major global bleaching event (El Niño)
- **2010-2020:** Accelerating bleaching frequency (Great Barrier Reef: 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022)
- **2023-2024:** Record-breaking ocean temperatures, widespread bleaching
- **2025:** Tipping point officially recognized as crossed

**Timeline Analysis:**
- **Threshold crossing:** Late 2010s to early 2020s (gradual, not instant)
- **Recognition lag:** ~5-10 years from crossing to official declaration
- **Manifestation period:** ~20-30 years from initial degradation to systemic collapse

**Validation for Simulation:**
This empirically validates the **multi-decadal timescale** approach in `climate_collapse_timelines_20251026.md`. Coral reefs are one of the "fast" tipping elements, and even they took 20-30 years to collapse after initial stress. This supports month-to-year scale transitions for fast elements, decades for intermediate, centuries for slow.

## 2. AMOC-Amazon Interaction: Nuanced Findings (June 2025)

### Competing Effects on Amazon Rainfall

**Citation:** International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis (IIASA). (2025). "AMOC decline increases rainfall in parts of the Amazon rainforest." June 2025.
**URL:** https://iiasa.ac.at/news/jun-2025/amoc-decline-increases-rainfall-in-parts-amazon-rainforest

**Key Finding:**
Weakening Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) may have a **stabilizing effect** on Southern Amazon rainfall during dry season, partially buffering rainfall loss from other climate drivers.

**Mechanism:**
- **AMOC weakening** → Shifts tropical Atlantic precipitation patterns
- **Northern Amazon:** Decreased rainfall (consistent with previous studies)
- **Southern Amazon:** INCREASED rainfall during dry season (new finding)
- **Net effect:** Regional heterogeneity, not uniform drying

**Limitation:**
> "This stabilizing interaction competes with other effects from deforestation and increasing temperatures, which would cause continued Amazon drying that the interaction will not be able to compensate for long-term."

**Implication for Simulation:**
- **AMOC-Amazon interaction is NOT uniformly negative**
- Regional variation matters (northern vs. southern Amazon)
- Temporary buffering can delay but not prevent tipping
- **Multiple stressors compound:** AMOC + deforestation + warming → eventual tipping despite local buffering

### Northern Amazon: High Vulnerability

**Citation:** Various 2024 studies on Heinrich Events and AMOC paleo-evidence
**Finding:** Northern Amazon regions are "massively impacted" even under moderate AMOC changes

**Historical Evidence:**
During Heinrich Events (past AMOC collapses):
- Dramatic decline in rainforest vegetation in northern Amazon
- Southern regions showed more resilience

**Implication:**
Amazon dieback is likely to be **spatially heterogeneous**, with northern regions tipping first, potentially triggering cascades to southern regions over longer timescales.

**DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-023-06970-0
**Publication:** February 2024

**Key Finding:**
By 2050, **10-47% of Amazon forest will be exposed to compounding disturbances** that may trigger unexpected ecosystem transitions:
- Rising temperatures
- Extreme droughts
- Deforestation
- Fire frequency

**Transition Outcome:**
"Large swathes of lush rainforest shifting to dry savannah" over **just decades** after tipping point crossed.

**Critical Threshold:**
> "Staying within 1.5°C of global warming is considered a 'safe boundary' for the Amazon forest to avoid large-scale transformations."

**Current Risk Assessment (2025):**
- Deforestation: ~17-20% (approaching 20-25% threshold from earlier research)
- Warming: ~1.4°C (exceeding 1.5°C "safe boundary" without rapid mitigation)
- Combined stressors: **HIGH RISK** of tipping within 25-50 years

## 7. Key Takeaways for Simulation

**1. First Empirical Tipping Point Provides Calibration**
Coral reef collapse (2020-2025) is the first contemporary tipping point to be crossed and recognized, providing a real-world benchmark for:
- **Timescale validation** (20-30 years for "fast" tipping)
- **Gradual vs. abrupt** (gradual threshold crossing, accelerating collapse)
- **Recognition lag** (5-10 years from crossing to scientific consensus)

**2. Multiple Tipping Points Approaching Simultaneously**
2025 is a critical juncture:
- **1 crossed** (coral)
- **2-3 at high risk** (Amazon, AMOC, Arctic ice)
- **1 ongoing** (permafrost)
- **2 committed** (WAIS, GIS on century timescales)

**3. Cascading Interactions Are Complex**
- AMOC-Amazon interaction has regional heterogeneity (not simple negative feedback)
- Temporary buffering effects can delay but not prevent tipping
- Multiple stressors compound (warming + deforestation + AMOC changes)

**4. No Month-Scale Collapse**
- Coral reefs: 20-30 years from stress to collapse
- Amazon: 30-50 years projected from current stress to savannah transition
- AMOC: 50-150 years transition if threshold crossed
- **Validates existing simulation rejection of instant month-scale collapse**

**5. Uncertainty Remains on Exact Timing**
- **Amazon:** 10-47% at risk by 2050 (wide range)
- **AMOC:** < 2°C threshold, but exact value uncertain (1.4-2.0°C plausible)
- **Cascades:** How fast does one tipping trigger others? (decades, not months/years)
