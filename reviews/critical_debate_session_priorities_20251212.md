## Executive Summary

**Overall Assessment: Priorities are PARTIALLY aligned with goals - but with significant blind spots.**

The simulation has impressive coverage of climate systems, AI capabilities, and tipping points. However, my review reveals:

1. **We're over-invested in environmental modeling** relative to social/political dynamics that actually drive outcomes
2. **Key parameters still rely on derived estimates** rather than empirical data (sleeper agent rates, sandbagging levels)

The research quality is excellent on what we've validated. The question is whether we're validating the right things.

### NOT Modeled (Critical Gaps)

#### 1. Supply Chain Dynamics
**Criticality:** HIGH
**Why it matters:** Post-alignment technology deployment requires global supply chains. COVID showed 30-40% disruption from modest shocks. We model technology effectiveness but not manufacturing/distribution bottlenecks.

**Research available:** Yes - World Bank (2023), McKinsey (2024) supply chain resilience studies.

**Recommendation:** Add supply chain fragility modifier to technology deployment effectiveness.

#### 2. Cyber Warfare Cascade Effects
**Criticality:** HIGH
**Why it matters:** We have `cyberSecurity.ts` but no modeling of coordinated cyber attacks on critical infrastructure (grid, finance, communications). AI capability growth increases both attack and defense surfaces.

**Research available:** Yes - CISA (2024), CyberPeace Institute (2024).

**Currently:** CyberSecurityPhase exists but focuses on AI compute security, not infrastructure.

#### 3. Authoritarian Tech Lock-In
**Criticality:** CRITICAL
**Why it matters:** Current dystopia progression tracks surveillance and oppression, but doesn't model how AI-enhanced authoritarianism becomes self-reinforcing. China's AI governance model is not represented as an alternative pathway.

**Research available:** Yes - Freedom House (2024), V-Dem (2024) digital authoritarianism indices.

**Currently:** GovernanceSystemPhase uses democracy index but doesn't model authoritarian stability dynamics.

#### 4. Information Ecosystem Collapse
**Criticality:** HIGH
**Why it matters:** `informationEcology.ts` exists but we don't model the full epistemic collapse scenario: AI-generated content flood -> trust collapse -> coordination failure. This is the information-age equivalent of climate tipping.

**Research available:** Yes - Benkler (2018), Wardle & Derakhshan (2024), MIT Media Lab studies.

**Currently:** Basic epistemic health metrics, no cascade modeling.

#### 5. Labor Market Transition Dynamics
**Criticality:** HIGH
**Why it matters:** `aiAssistedSkills/laborDistribution.ts` exists but doesn't model the political economy of displacement: retraining failure rates, geographic concentration, political radicalization from unemployment.

**Research available:** Yes - Acemoglu & Restrepo (2020, 2022, 2024), OECD (2024).

**Currently:** Basic labor distribution, no feedback to political instability.

#### 6. Resource Nationalism
**Criticality:** MEDIUM
**Why it matters:** Rare earth elements, lithium, copper - the materials for green transition are geographically concentrated. Resource wars could block deployment.

**Research available:** Yes - IEA Critical Minerals (2024), USGS (2024).

**Currently:** Not explicitly modeled beyond generic "resource scarcity" multipliers.

## Part 4: Current Simulation Assumptions - What Could Be Wrong?

### Assumption 1: Climate and AI Are Separate Systems

**Current model:** Climate systems run largely independently from AI capability growth. Energy budget creates some coupling.

**Challenge:** In reality, AI compute growth competes directly with climate mitigation for:
- Clean energy capacity
- Rare earth materials
- Engineering talent
- Government attention/funding

**Potential flaw:** Our model may understate the resource competition between AI development and climate response.

**Evidence:** IEA (2025) projects AI datacenters consuming 4.5% of global electricity by 2030, competing with electrification and DAC.

### Assumption 2: Governance Quality Is Exogenous

**Current model:** Government quality follows slow evolution based on democracy index, elections, crises.

**Challenge:** AI directly transforms governance capability through:
- Enhanced policy analysis
- Better coordination tools
- Surveillance and control capabilities
- Disinformation and manipulation

**Potential flaw:** We may be underestimating both AI-enhanced governance effectiveness AND AI-enabled authoritarianism.

### Assumption 3: Technology Deployment Is Skill-Limited

**Current model:** Deployment effectiveness scales with coordination quality and energy availability.

**Challenge:** Technologies also face:
- NIMBY opposition (wind farms, nuclear)
- Regulatory capture (fossil fuel lobbying)
- Path dependency (infrastructure lock-in)
- Behavioral resistance (dietary change, mobility patterns)

**Potential flaw:** We may be optimistic about technology deployment even with perfect coordination.

### Assumption 4: AI Alignment Is Binary

**Current model:** AIs are either aligned or misaligned, with continuous sandbagging/deception metrics.

**Challenge:** Real alignment is multi-dimensional:
- Aligned with whose values? (different humans disagree)
- Aligned for what timescale? (short vs long-term)
- Aligned under what conditions? (normal vs adversarial)
- Corrigibly aligned vs autonomously aligned?

**Potential flaw:** Our binary model may miss "aligned but problematic" scenarios (Midas problem, Goodhart's Law).

### Assumption 5: Recovery Timescales Are Knowable

**Current model:** Recovery from catastrophes follows S-curves with research-backed timescales.

**Challenge:** We have NO empirical data for:
- Recovery from 70%+ population loss
- Recovery from multiple simultaneous tipping points
- Recovery under AI-mediated governance
- Recovery with degraded knowledge infrastructure

**Potential flaw:** Our recovery models are necessarily speculative for scenarios we care most about.

### Recommendation 4: Add Uncertainty Bounds to Fundamental Assumptions

For each major assumption, we should:
1. Document the assumption explicitly
2. Identify contradictory research
3. Model with uncertainty ranges, not point estimates
4. Run sensitivity analysis on assumption violations

## Research Gaps Requiring Validation

### HIGH Priority (Blocks Key Features)

1. **Governance cascade dynamics:** Political science equivalent of tipping point research. How do governance failures cascade? What's the equivalent of "threshold lowering" for institutions?

2. **AI alignment failure modes post-deployment:** Current research focuses on pre-deployment detection. What happens when aligned AI becomes misaligned through:
   - Distributional shift
   - Goal drift
   - Adversarial manipulation
   - Unintended value lock-in

3. **Multi-scale coordination failure:** How do local coordination failures aggregate to global dysfunction? Current model treats coordination as single scalar.

### MEDIUM Priority (Improves Realism)

4. **Technology deployment political economy:** Why do beneficial technologies fail to deploy even when technically ready? (Nuclear power, GMOs, carbon pricing)

5. **Social trust collapse/restoration asymmetry:** Is trust really restored in 24-36 months as BCG claims? Or is there permanent damage?

6. **Climate-security nexus:** How do climate impacts drive conflict escalation? Current model has basic resource scarcity multipliers but lacks nuanced pathways.

### LOW Priority (Refinement)

7. **Regional heterogeneity in AI adoption:** Different regions adopt AI at different rates for cultural/political reasons. Currently homogeneous.

8. **Intergenerational dynamics:** How do climate impacts affect birth rates, migration, and human capital formation across generations?
