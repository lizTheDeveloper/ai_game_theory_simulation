### 5. Methodological Issues

**Is it valid to apply voluntary adoption curves to forced/crisis adaptation?**

**ABSOLUTELY NOT.**

Rogers' model has fundamental assumptions that break in crisis:

**Assumption 1: Voluntary choice**
- Rogers studied farmers choosing seeds, doctors choosing drugs
- Job displacement removes choice - adapt or become obsolete
- Forced adoption creates fundamentally different dynamics

**Assumption 2: Pro-innovation bias**
- Rogers assumes innovations benefit adopters
- AI/automation may HARM workers (job loss)
- Rational response is resistance, not adoption

**Assumption 3: Normal economic conditions**
- Rogers' bell curve assumes stable environment
- Crises create discontinuous change
- Standard distributions don't apply to black swan events

**Assumption 4: Social system stability**
- Rogers requires functioning social networks for diffusion
- Job displacement disrupts social systems
- Opinion leaders (early adopters) may lose influence if unemployed

### 6. Base Rate Analysis

**What percentage of workers actually persist in adopting during unemployment?**

Available data suggests **MUCH LOWER than 5%:**

**Empirical Base Rates:**
- 26% of US workers used generative AI at work (late 2024) - but this is pre-crisis
- During high unemployment, adoption likely falls to survival minimums
- Historical automation waves show worker resistance, not adoption floors

**Better estimates for crisis adoption:**
- 0-2% might continue adoption if directly beneficial to survival
- Most adoption would be FORCED (employer mandated), not voluntary
- True voluntary adoption during job crisis: likely <1%

**The 5% floor is therefore 5-10x too high based on crisis evidence.**
