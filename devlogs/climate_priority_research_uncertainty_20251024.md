## Executive Summary

Two competing research narratives emerged from agent collaboration:

**OPTIMISTIC (Green Growth):** Policy mixes work, synergies exist, climate action boosts economy
**PESSIMISTIC (Structural Barriers):** 96% of policies fail, vested interests dominant, carbon leakage

Both are grounded in peer-reviewed research. Rather than picking sides, we'll model BOTH to bracket uncertainty and test which mechanisms dominate simulation outcomes.

- IRENA-ILO Employment Review 2024: 16.2M green jobs

### Key Claims

**Priority Allocation:**
- Current progressive governments: 15-25% climate priority (Biden, von der Leyen)
- Maximum observed: 30-40% climate priority (Denmark, Germany)
- No government exceeds 40% while maintaining core functions

**Policy Effectiveness:**
- Policy mixes (carbon pricing + regulations + subsidies): **15-25% emission reduction** over 5 years
- Single instruments: 0-5% reduction (ineffective)
- Success rate: 4% of 1,500 policies achieved major reductions (63 policies)

**Green Growth:**
- Carbon pricing: -6.8% emissions (bias-corrected), GDP neutral to +0.3%
- Clean energy investment: $2T annually (+60% since Paris Agreement)
- Green jobs: 16.2M globally (+18% YoY growth)
- Economic co-benefits: +0.5-1.2% GDP from health, jobs, innovation

**Timeline Lags:**
- Policy implementation → emission reduction: 2-3 years
- Emission reduction → temperature benefit: 20-30 years
- Policy mix effectiveness: 5-8 years to reach full synergy

**Political Stability:**
- Election cycles: Every 4-6 years (democracies)
- Policy reversal risk: 30-50% per election (moderate), 50-70% (ambitious)

## Pessimistic Research Narrative (research-skeptic)

### Sources
- Hickel & Vogel (2023) Lancet Planetary Health: Decoupling debunked
- Nature Communications (2024): Historical CO2 reductions
- Parrique et al.: Green growth literature review
- Böhringer et al. (2022): EU policy interference analysis
- Fossil fuel lobbying data, carbon leakage studies

### Key Critiques

**1. Green Growth is Impossible**
- Hickel & Vogel (2023): At current "green growth" rates, high-income countries need **220+ years** to reduce emissions 95%
- Nature Communications (2024): **60% of historical CO2 reductions occurred during recessions**, not green growth
- Parrique et al.: Zero evidence of absolute resource decoupling at required scale globally
- **Implication:** Economic growth fundamentally incompatible with rapid decarbonization

**2. Policy Effectiveness Grossly Inflated**
- Stechemesser's 15-25% reduction is based on **top 4% of successful policies** (63 out of 1,500)
- **Median policy effectiveness:** 5-8% reduction, not 15-25%
- Real-world evidence: EU emissions only -1.5% annually despite €80/ton carbon price
- Global emissions **increased 1.1% in 2023** despite record renewable deployment
- **Implication:** Optimistic narrative cherry-picks rare successes, ignores 96% failures

**3. Synergy Multiplier is Fantasy**
- Böhringer et al. (2022): Overlapping EU policies **reduced effectiveness by 20-30%**
- Reality: Policy interactions create redundancy and interference, not synergy
- **Recommended multiplier:** 0.8-1.2x (policies interfere more than synergize)

**4. Timeline Lags Underestimated**
- Permitting + lawsuits + infrastructure: **8-15 years** for major projects, not 2-3 years
- Political resistance extends implementation indefinitely
- **Example:** Offshore wind approval in US took 12 years (first farm operational 2016, proposed 2004)

**5. Missing Critical Mechanisms**

**Political Economy (Vested Interests):**
- $30 trillion in stranded fossil assets
- Annual lobbying spend: $500M+ globally
- **Effect:** -30% policy effectiveness when threatening major industries

**Carbon Leakage:**
- Unilateral action has **30-50% leakage rate** without China/India participation
- EU carbon border adjustment only partially addresses (covers ~40% of emissions)

**Backlash Thresholds:**
- Policies collapse when energy costs exceed **10% of household budget**
- France gilets jaunes: carbon tax triggered mass protests at €65/ton
- **Political reversal probability:** 40-60%, not 30-50%

**Goodhart's Law:**
- Any metric targeted directly degrades 20-30% in validity
- Renewable deployment doesn't equal emission reduction (see Germany paradox)

### Recommended Parameters (Pessimistic)

| Scenario | Climate Priority | Expected Emission Reduction | Expected GDP Impact |
|----------|------------------|----------------------------|---------------------|
| Status Quo | 10% | 0% to -0.5%/year | +2-3%/year (baseline) |
| Moderate | 20% | -0.5% to -2%/year | +1.5-2.5%/year (modest co-benefits) |
| Ambitious | 30% (cap) | -2% to -4%/year | +0.5-1.5%/year (growing tensions) |
| Crisis-Mode | N/A (infeasible) | N/A | N/A |

**Key Adjustments:**
- Cap maximum priority at **30%** even in crisis (40-45% politically infeasible)
- Reduce all effectiveness parameters by **60-70%** (use median, not top 4%)
- Replace synergy multiplier with **interaction penalty:** 0.8-1.2x for >5 policies
- Add **vested interest resistance:** -30% effectiveness when threatening major industries
- Model **implementation gaps:** 8-15 years realistically
- **Carbon leakage:** -30-50% effectiveness without global coordination

## Simulation Strategy: Bracketing Uncertainty

Rather than picking one narrative, we'll test BOTH to understand:
1. Which research predictions match simulation behavior?
2. How sensitive are outcomes to parameter uncertainty?
3. What mechanisms dominate (synergies vs barriers)?

### Scenario Matrix (2×4 = 8 combinations)

**Research Frame:**
- **Frame A:** Optimistic (green growth, policy mixes work, synergies)
- **Frame B:** Pessimistic (structural barriers, vested interests, interference)

**Government Priority:**
- **Priority 1:** Status Quo (10% climate)
- **Priority 2:** Moderate (20% climate)
- **Priority 3:** Ambitious (30-35% climate)
- **Priority 4:** Crisis-Mode (40-45% climate) - optimistic only

**Total simulations:** 8 scenarios × 5 runs each = 40 Monte Carlo runs

### Expected Insights

**If Optimistic Frame Dominates:**
- Ambitious priority → Paris-aligned emission reductions (-4-6%/year)
- Green jobs → economic growth synergies visible
- Crisis-mode feasible and effective

**If Pessimistic Frame Dominates:**
- All scenarios → modest emission reductions (0-4%/year max)
- Economic tensions emerge at 30%+ priority
- Crisis-mode triggers political collapse

**If Mixed (Most Likely):**
- Optimistic mechanisms work at low priority (20%)
- Pessimistic barriers dominate at high priority (35%+)
- Reveals threshold where structural barriers overwhelm policy effectiveness

## Philosophical Note: Why Test Both?

This project's research philosophy is **"let the model show what it shows"** rather than tuning for desired outcomes. By testing both optimistic and pessimistic parameter sets:

1. **We honor competing research traditions** (growth optimism vs ecological economics)
2. **We reveal which mechanisms dominate** in complex system dynamics
3. **We provide uncertainty bounds** for policy effectiveness
4. **We avoid confirmation bias** by not pre-selecting the "correct" narrative

If the simulation shows green growth is impossible even with optimistic parameters, that's valuable. If it shows structural barriers are overstated, that's equally valuable. The truth emerges from simulation behavior, not researcher priors.
